# AVARENNE
## Volume I — The Waking House
### Chapter 40 — The Height of the Water

The first bucket spilled water on Gaston’s shoe. The second struck Elias’s leg before he had even decided which way to move.

“If you stay there, we’ll get the rest of you wet too,” a woman behind him said.

Elias flattened himself against the wall. The worker passed with a bucket in each hand, leaning just enough to keep them from scraping her skirt. The Mutual’s courtyard smelled of damp wood and cloth dried without enough air. Spilled water ran between two stones to a drain.

Noemi Darel was waiting beside an empty vessel.

“You found the entrance.”

“We made ourselves known,” Gaston said, looking at his shoe.

She handed him a cloth. It was not clean enough for a wound, but cleaner than the leather he needed to dry. Elias followed it with his eyes and realized that she had already moved a stool out of the way.

“This isn’t a fountain for the city,” Noemi said. “First I’d like to stop sending people up and down with those.”

She meant the buckets, not the visitors. Elias managed not to ask.

Parts of prostheses were laid out on the bench behind her: a hinge polished by movement, an open leather strap, a hollow section that reminded him of a calf. A board set on edge divided them from the dirty objects. A closed parcel occupied the end nearest the door.

Elias put down his folder only when Noemi showed him the clear space.

“We’ve come about the route for the water,” Gaston said. “Yselle gave us the drawing.”

“I’ll give you the real thing.”

She led them past the bench and through a doorway so low that Gaston ducked for safety. Elias copied him half a step later. The back of the courtyard opened onto service stairs and a walkway. Higher up, a tank rested on supports, a drop creeping down its rim.

Below stood a larger basin that could be reached from the courtyard without steps.

“The service water comes in here. We take it up in buckets. Then it comes down where it’s needed.”

Noemi explained where: washing tools, cleaning the depot, tasks needing a steady supply modest enough to seem unimportant to anyone who could turn on a tap somewhere else.

Elias followed the visible pipe. One section had lost its paint; another had been painted around a fastening instead of being dismantled. He stopped where it went beneath the paving.

“From here?”

“It goes underneath.”

Gaston marked the limit of what they could see on the plan. Elias put a hand against the wall and reached for the distance between the courtyard floor and the tank’s rim.

The height came clearly, separate from the effort of climbing it. For an instant the stretch hidden by the walkway ceased to keep him from knowing the relationship between the two points. He sensed his position below and the rim above, like the two ends of the same exercise.

Then he took his hand away. The weight of his own body once again seemed quite different from a number.

Gaston set the reference and began the ordinary check. Noemi held the end of the tape while Elias wrote. A drop from the tank landed on his wrist; he moved the paper.

“Does it leak?”

“When we fill it too much.”

“How do you know when it’s full?”

She pointed to a place on the walkway.

From there, they could look inside. The level the water needed to reach had left a mark. Elias looked at the stairs, the mark, then the hands of the worker returning with an empty bucket.

The pump would save someone a journey. It could also fill the tank too much, much faster.

“We’ll need to see the level from below,” he said.

Gaston had him note it beside the first problem.

“And reach the shutoff,” Noemi added.

“Here?”

She took him to the side of the old pipe. The control needed a short key; it was not a crank anyone could find with a hand in the dark. The key lay on a nearby shelf, high enough not to be buried among things left on the ground.

Noemi picked it up, showed him the socket and put it back.

“Whoever uses it needs to know where it is. I don’t want the last person who finishes taking it away in a pocket.”

Elias looked at the shelf. A small lip kept things from falling, but the key could disappear under a cloth.

“Do we put this on the drawing?” he asked Gaston.

“First we ask whether it stays here.”

“It stays here,” Noemi said. “Until we find a better place.”

She did not sound as though she were making an eternal promise. Elias made a separate mark to remind himself. The distance from the key to the socket was short. Still, his ability to reach an object across a little space was not enough: whoever worked there after them would need to find it.

A voice in the courtyard asked whether a load could come through. Noemi called back to wait and moved them aside.

A man arrived pushing a small cart. At first Elias noticed only how he held the weight back while walking backward. Then he saw the leg: one knee did not bend with the other. The foot met the stone with a harder sound.

The cart fitted between the bench and the tank support. Just. The man still had to bring his hip against the wall, seeking a place where he could pull without losing his balance.

Elias reached for the handle.

“Which side?” he asked.

The man showed him, and together they passed the narrowing. Elias felt the load try to turn. He straightened it with his elbow close to his body, without suddenly making it light.

Once the cart was in the wider courtyard, his companion signaled that he could let go.

Noemi had stayed beside the narrow stretch.

“The machine was here on the drawing,” she said.

Elias opened the plan. The base’s rectangle occupied part of the passage they had just used. From above there seemed to be room: room for a man without a cart, able to bend both knees and turn whenever he pleased.

He repositioned the sheet against the wall. The paper offered no other solution out of gratitude.

“If we put it farther along, can people reach the bench?”

Noemi had him walk the route.

The first position prevented a door from opening. The second brought the base close to the basin’s edge. Gaston measured and made Elias return to the starting point.

For a while they found nothing that worked. The woman with the buckets passed twice, smiling openly the second time.

“At least now you know how long it takes us.”

Elias looked at the reddened hands on the handle.

“Some of it.”

He tried imagining his own body with one movement missing. The maneuver he had made with the cart would not have worked the same way. He went to the opposite wall and found a position they had not yet marked.

“The pipe is longer here.”

Gaston looked in the proposed direction. Noemi opened the nearby door all the way and slowly closed it.

“But we don’t lose this entrance.”

She placed a stool at the edge of the proposed base and went back toward the bench. First she passed alone, then had the empty cart brought through. The same man pushed it without lowering the load or seeking the wall with his hip.

Elias noted the clearance. It was a proposal to take back to Yselle; the height and length of the new section had to be compared with what the machine could do. At least they would not be discussing a pump set in a passage that did not exist.

Before leaving the courtyard to follow the pipe, Gaston asked where he could get a drink. Elias’s throat was dry enough for him to look straight at the vessel they had just filled.

Noemi followed his gaze, took a clean glass from the cupboard and pointed to the inner doorway.

“That water is for work.”

“I wasn’t saying—”

“I saw you looking. People who’ve been here for years do it too.”

He followed her to a jug kept on a separate shelf. The glass reached him full of water, without his having to defend where he had looked. He drank too quickly; when he put it down, Noemi filled it halfway and showed him where to leave it afterward.

“When the pump works, people see water coming out of a pipe. They don’t see the line on your drawing.”

Elias picked up the plan again. He marked which circuit would feed the tank and asked Gaston to check the wording before delivery. Then he finished the second half glass slowly. He already knew he was thirsty, and this seemed a good opportunity to deal with it before measuring again.

Gaston went off with the attendant who knew the circuit. Elias stayed to finish the sketch.

Noemi picked up a small worn hinge from the bench and held it between her fingers while looking at the drawing.

“The hearing?” she asked.

He raised his head.

“It’s over. I can work outside again.”

“I can see that.”

He managed a smile. She put the hinge back in its little tray. A few letters marked a label; Elias looked away before reading them as a question addressed to him too.

“Can that be replaced?”

“Yes. That’s the good news.”

Noemi showed him the worn place without handing him the object. Two surfaces had worked against each other until the edge had developed obvious play.

“The bad news is that what’s meant to arrive doesn’t always arrive when it’s needed. Meanwhile someone waits. And they can’t always stay still.”

Elias thought of the pump’s liner. A replaceable part had value beyond the person who knew how to draw it.

“Yselle wants the replacements to be made here.”

“So do I. Then we have to find someone who knows how to make them and pay for the work.”

She was not asking him to invent a workshop from the bench where they had put a drawing. She set down the hinge and moved a strip of leather away from the small dirty basin.

Something fell in the inner room. Noemi turned, listened to a reply and returned to her place. Elias had already risen.

“A tool,” she said. “It happens to us too.”

He put his pen back on the paper.

Gaston returned with the references for the visible section. Beneath the paving, some points remained to be checked before a different connection could be put to work. Gaston marked those points on the drawing. They would return before connecting the pump.

As they gathered their tools, Noemi asked Elias to carry the empty vessel to the bench where it was needed. He took one handle and waited for the worker to offer him the other.

“It isn’t full,” she said.

“I can feel that.”

“Then you can walk faster.”

He followed her across the courtyard. For once he had the lighter load and a route already chosen by someone else. They put it down with its rim facing the wall, where it would not collect dirty water.

Noemi approved the sketch of the clear position. Gaston explained that Yselle would need to check it against her own drawings. She nodded and retrieved the cloth from the stone where she had left it.

On the way out, Elias saw the worker return to the two buckets. One was still wet. She picked it up, checked the handle and went to the basin.

He opened his notebook again while Gaston said goodbye. He drew the route she kept taking, a line rising and falling twice. He did not need to ask anyone how good it would be to cross it out.
