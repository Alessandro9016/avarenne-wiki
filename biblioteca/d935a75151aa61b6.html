# AVARENNE
## Volume I — The Waking House
### Chapter 33 — Vern's Signature

Vern arrived at the workshop on the twenty-ninth with his own account.

Elias saw him from the window and thought, before anything else, that the man might still withdraw his admission. He hated himself for the relief he would feel at being able to be merely angry again.

Marcel let the lot supervisor in and called Gaston, who was already working nearby. He did not summon a crowd.

Vern rested the sheet on the bench.

“I want to check this says the same thing.”

Gaston arrived drying his hands on a cloth. He did not ask whether Vern still intended to stand by his words. He greeted them and sought somewhere to read alongside the others.

The lot supervisor opened the sheet and turned it toward the bench. A fold crossed a line; Elias had to hold it with a finger to keep a word from disappearing in the reflected light.

“Is this the version you want to take?”

“That's why I came.”

Elias wanted to ask why Gaston's account was not enough if Vern had already confirmed every sentence. From the care with which Vern followed the text, he understood that writing in the first person required another choice. He would not reward him for taking longer. But he wanted to know what he was truly willing to sign.

Marcel removed his tool from the bench. He improvised no seat of honour for the visitor. He cleared enough space that they need not read among objects forcing someone to hold the sheet throughout.

Elias smelled the damp cloth beside the vise. The detail was unimportant, yet helped him remain present while the man he had feared for weeks placed his account beside the measurements.

They began at the first line. Gaston followed the passages in his notebook with a finger. He had not hidden the verification's account to make correct wording harder to find; neither would he let Vern make it seem a different decision.

The opening lines named the first measurement, the decision to move the bench, and the reuse of the certification. Gaston compared them with what he had recorded during the check on the twenty-sixth. Elias read one sentence twice.

“This leaves out that you knew the calibration needed repeating.”

Vern looked at the sheet.

“I wrote that I relied on the margin.”

“That's not the same thing.”

The sound of files continued elsewhere in the workshop. Someone had not interrupted work to make their discussion more important.

Vern sat. He removed his jacket and folded it on his knees with a care Elias had never noticed during inspections.

“If I write it like that, I can't say it was a simple misunderstanding anymore.”

Elias felt the absurd injustice of having to be patient with someone who had had time to choose before the accident. He did not have to wield patience as a weapon to appear better.

“It wasn't.”

Vern stayed still. Then he asked for a pen.

Vern held the pen without writing. Its point stayed near the line Elias had indicated, and nobody told him where to put the sentence without making the page untidier.

“I have people who expect me to keep work moving,” he murmured.

Gaston left the notebook open. Elias thought of answering that he did too. It would return them to a contest in which Vern's wrongdoing must be large enough to dwarf every other fear.

“You're not being asked to say you didn't know your job,” he said instead. “You're being asked which choice you made knowing what was missing.”

The man lowered his eyes. His cuff slipped over the hand holding the paper and he jerked it back. He no longer resembled a supervisor distributing duties without pause; he seemed someone trying to avoid a cost that had reached his name.

“They'll read this sentence without me there too.”

“Yes,” Marcel said. “That's why we're checking it.”

The answer needed no threat. Vern could not accompany the page everywhere to alter its tone. If he wanted understanding without denying what he had known, he must find wording he could support now.

Elias waited for him to write. He did not feel the satisfaction he had imagined at watching a hand tremble over a confession. The handwriting stayed steady enough. Acknowledging responsibility did not need to look humiliating to be real.

When the pen moved, Gaston shifted just far enough to cast no shadow. That gesture too left the sentence to the person who had chosen to add it.

The correction was brief. The page looked less clean than before. Elias preferred that version.

Gaston reread the passage, and Vern confirmed it. The signature did not alter April twenty-first. It altered what that man was prepared to maintain before others now.

“I'll take it to the hearing myself,” he said.

“We'll bring our checks too,” Marcel replied.

Vern nodded without protesting at the distrust. His promise on the twenty-sixth had not become a right to make every further corroboration disappear.

Etienne Brac arrived later, notified for the financial discussion. He had a list of unpaid days and a question far simpler than the language of the original offer.

“Does this part still depend on what I sign about the accident?”

Vern looked at the separate sheets on the bench.

“I've asked for it to be handled separately.”

“Who decides the payment?”

The lot supervisor identified the office that would arrange the balance. He did not claim the right to withdraw funds from a till that was not his. He said he would confirm the shifts and dispute an account that made them disappear.

Etienne had one day corrected. Gaston checked the available reference. They did not need an Effigy to find the right count.

Elias stayed beside the bench. His own rehabilitation did not coincide with that man's entitlement to the money, and at last he could remain in the same room without trying to join the two to feel safer.

Etienne compared his list with the shift reference. It took a moment to recognize a day entered in the wrong place. He said so without first looking at Elias, as though he needed no permission to make the account more precise just when everything seemed to be concluding.

Gaston found the reference. Vern checked and accepted the correction. He offered no different sum because the first page had changed; they were discussing lost work, not the price of a sentence.

“I want to know what I'll sign when I pay,” the worker said, then corrected himself with a dry smile. “When I'm paid.”

The laughter was brief but freed the room from remaining solemn until the end. Elias understood even a verbal mistake could be left to its maker without becoming a sign of confusion about everything else.

Marcel had him read the distinction between receipt and reconstruction of the accident. Etienne followed the line and asked which office would arrange payment. Vern indicated the step he could confirm and did not promise to arrive with the money himself.

Elias stayed beside them. He could have added gratitude to make acceptance easier; that was not what the worker asked. He was seeking a way home with an owed service finally recognized and the right to remember what happened without selling it with his signature.

Once they checked the copies, Elias moved the inkwell away from Vern's elbow. The man still held the pen. There was nothing further to add to the numbers before beginning the next sentence.

When the texts had been separated and copied, Vern remained with the pen in his hand.

“I didn't want you hurt,” he told Etienne.

The worker looked at him.

“I believe that.”

Vern seemed to breathe more easily too soon.

“It doesn't change that you made me work without the check that was needed.”

The man lowered the pen.

“No.”

They did not shake hands. Etienne did not owe the lot supervisor an ending beautiful enough to recount as forgiveness.

Elias escorted both to the door. Vern paused before the strip of wood Marcel was working on.

“It's crooked here,” he said, indicating a point with the precision of someone who knew how to look.

Marcel checked.

“Yes.”

The lot supervisor looked at the crooked point of the slat as Marcel turned it in the light. The observation's precision had not put everything else right. Elias felt how easy it would be to want him wrong even here, to avoid keeping competence and responsibility together.

Marcel checked the edge and agreed. He did not thank him so solemnly as to make the conversation seem to have ended well for everyone. He had acknowledged a fact and would use it to correct the piece.

Vern put on his jacket. The signed sheet was no longer the only object requiring attention, but it could not become a closed matter merely because someone had found a way to discuss the trade again.

“See you on the thirty-first,” he said.

Elias nodded. The date already existed in his notebook; hearing the other man say it restored the moment when they must actually appear.

Etienne stood a step beyond the door. He had found his cap's comfortable position and looked toward work still to do before returning home. He was not waiting for Vern to offer a final sentence good enough for forgiveness.

Elias realized the confrontation could end without an emotional settlement: a correct signature, an account to settle, relationships that did not regain their previous form. None became false because the gesture that would make them look harmonious was missing.

He moved aside to clear the passage. Cart noise came from the street. It would receive both without assigning each the music Elias might imagine. He stayed by the threshold, letting them choose when to go. The master still held the piece.

Vern left. His mentor took up the piece again and did not erase that observation merely because it had come from the wrong man.

Gaston put the copy in its agreed place. Elias helped clear the bench without reopening the sheet to check the signature. He had seen it signed; they would take it to the hearing with the other verifications. For that afternoon, responsibility could be kept without reading it every few minutes.

Later Elias returned to the signed sheet. For weeks he had sought evidence that would let him breathe. The evidence was there, and it had not given back his lost nights.

It had given back a concrete possibility of speaking about his work without beginning with the defense of his name.

Marcel asked whether he wanted to reread any part. Elias shook his head. The sheet would remain available after the afternoon's work; he need not keep watching to prevent it changing.

He took it for what it was. Then he closed the folder and returned to his work at the bench.
