# AVARENNE
## Volume I — The Waking House
### Chapter 30 — The Object That Did Not Leave

The sample left the workshop on the morning of the twenty-seventh.

Elias watched it disappear into the same ordinary delivery process for which Marcel had required a name, a receipt, and return conditions. The Surveyor's Hinge did not accompany it. No second parcel left with enough weight to let someone claim a misunderstanding.

Marcel had the carrier check that he was receiving only the agreed sample. Elias stayed near the bench, far enough away not to make his attention look like an attempt to reclaim what they had agreed to return.

The parcel had an ordinary shape. Its cloth suggested none of the questions they had laid on that metal since its arrival. When the man lifted it, Elias expected relief. Instead, he felt tempted to hold it until they knew more.

It was not their apparatus. It had not been delivered for ever. He had good reason to want to understand exactly what these people were asking of them, but the fact that he wanted to understand did not grant him the right to keep someone else's property in his custody indefinitely.

Marcel rechecked the delivery reference and let the parcel leave. He did not stage the refusal as proof of superiority. The man carrying it was not the recipient of their questions about Helren's note.

Elias followed him with his eyes until he reached the street. A few steps would lose him among other loads. If Elias followed, the world would offer no corridor reserved for investigation: there would be carts, people, unfamiliar turns, and a job to which he must explain his absence.

He returned to the bench. The surface beneath the sample was cleaner than the rest, a pale outline where it had rested. He studied it briefly, then wiped the whole area. The object could leave without their memory of what they had observed depending on a stain.

Ilian had carried the reply and received the commercial contact's answer. He stopped to reread it with them, without granting it the courtesy of seeming more precise at each reading.

The text proposed a demonstration in a depot outside the lot. It discussed moving people between points to be specified by the client after the component arrived. It took for granted a choice of destination no one at the workshop had offered.

Marcel read it and put it down.

“They aren't describing the object they sent.”

“Or a service we promised,” Elias said.

Livia would read that passage when she arrived. Elias marked it with a strip of paper: points specified after arrival. The client wanted to choose the destination only once someone else had delivered the means.

He looked at Ilian. The man had begun folding the letter along a worn crease. He would have to carry it from one table to another yet again.

“Can I close the part concerning my drawing?” Ilian asked.

Marcel indicated the exchanged texts. The requested drawing had been prepared; unverified properties remained unverified. They did not need to consent to a demonstration to describe work already performed.

“The balance hasn't arrived,” the draftsman said.

Elias was about to assure him it would when he realized he had no reason to know.

“I can help you keep the jobs separate,” he said instead.

Ilian nodded. It did not seem a great consolation, but he could use it.

They took a sheet and separated what had been requested from what had been delivered. The reproduction did not become an expert report; Helren's comparison did not become a signature authorizing the client's demonstration. The reply named Mael Vaur as the contact without inventing his role at every stage.

Elias wanted to follow the sample to the depot, hidden well enough not to be seen, able at last to learn who owned the question.

The thought lasted until he imagined himself outside the workshop, his permit still restricted and no one informed of his route.

It would not be courage. It would hand the first curious interlocutor precisely the isolation he lacked.

“I'm not following the delivery,” he said.

Marcel raised an eyebrow.

“I hadn't asked you to.”

“I was asking myself.”

“A dangerous conversation. Try to have a witness.”

Ilian laughed quietly. Then he gathered his correspondence and requested a copy of Marcel's statement confirming the work performed on site.

Ilian counted the pages before putting them in his bag. He was still missing payment, not another explanation of what he had delivered.

“I'm not asking you to pay in his place,” he told Marcel.

“That's not what I'm offering.”

“Good. I'd hate to have to thank you while I'm still angry with him.”

Elias lifted the ruler from where the draftsman needed to put his case. Ilian set down the bag, sought the copy, and began counting again. This time Elias actually helped.

“What if they answer again?” he asked.

“Send it,” Marcel said. “If they answer, we read it first.”

“I have to go there about payment.”

The master looked at Ilian's bag. “You don't need to bring one of our components for that.”

The draftsman nodded. Elias removed the weight from the paper's edge; Ilian rolled it before he could add another promise.

The draftsman nodded. He had time to return to work if they stopped discussing hypotheses. Elias cleared his way and picked up a ruler that would prevent him setting his bag down comfortably.

Staying in the workshop after the object left proved harder than expected. His mind wanted to follow the parcel, see who opened the case, recognize the client's name in the handwriting. His hands could only move what remained and prepare material for the hearing.

Elias chose a job to finish before Livia arrived. What he needed was something with a measure he could actually reach, rather than another reason to treat every question that aroused his curiosity as an urgent matter. Marcel indicated a housing to check. Elias took it and felt the newly worked edge beneath his thumb.

After he had left, Livia arrived to check the material intended for the hearing. Elias showed her the commercial text only within the limits already authorized.

“We don't know who rewrote the note,” he said.

“You still know that better than when you wanted a name that could explain everything.”

“It doesn't seem very glorious progress.”

Livia set her case on the bench.

“You could try useful progress.”

Once again they separated the bench file from the commercial one. It had not become a ritual capable of magically protecting people. It merely let them take the right questions to the thirty-first instead of a theory about every evil in the city.

Elias paused before the key he did not own.

The room's ordinary door remained usable with its custodians' consent. One imbued opening was still available. There was no obligation to consume it to prove its worth to a client.

“If we really find someone who can recharge it...” he began.

“We'll ask what they want and what they can do,” Marcel said.

“Before telling them everything.”

“Before deciding how much they need to be told.”

Marcel asked what information they needed to share to assess a replenishment service. Elias began with the apparatus, its marks, and the remaining reserve; he stopped before adding every person who would want to use it.

“Those aren't needed to know whether the operator can do the work,” he said.

“Not immediately.”

The master did not turn confidentiality into an absolute impossibility of asking for help. Expertise nobody could encounter would replenish nothing. They needed to frame a request without handing over a catalog of their relationships too.

Livia indicated the commercial text. The description was generic where an assessment should begin, precise only in asking them to bring an object. Elias looked at his note and briefly found the risk of the same error: he wanted someone qualified but had not yet prepared a question that would let him recognize them.

“I can say what we aren't offering,” he murmured.

“And what are you asking for?”

The copyist's question forced him back to the sheet. Another prohibition would not make the required service legible. He tried describing it briefly, then reread it imagining someone unfamiliar with the place.

The first version was incomplete. The second seemed to promise a demonstration they had not agreed. Elias deleted that part and left the problem open. They need not hire someone before evening to prove the client's refusal had not blocked them.

Marcel put away the confirmation of return that had just arrived. One step had closed; the search for suitable expertise could continue without being driven by the haste of whoever had asked the wrong question.

Elias kept the sheet beside the commercial folder, separate from hearing documents. Future work would need its own order, not the emotional disorder left by what was nearing its conclusion.

The difference was small and less comfortable. Elias wrote it down.

Confirmation of the sample's return arrived that afternoon. Marcel attached it to the original delivery record. Their willingness to show it had ended; the client did not thereby own a right to every object that followed.

The housing had a recess its edge concealed at first glance. Elias laid his hand on the metal and used Near Coordinates to reach the nearby geometry the piece offered. The small change in level became a distinct relationship, unlike a shadow to interpret.

He checked the value with the ordinary instrument wherever it could reach. He possessed no rival truths, one magical and one professional. He had two ways to compare the same object, each with its conditions.

Marcel asked which side he intended to work next. Elias indicated the point, then stopped to explain why he would remove no material from the other edge. It was an answer he could still give without mentioning his restricted permit in every sentence.

The master listened and left him the piece. He did not reward him with absolute trust or find an artificial way to remind him of the first measurement's danger. There was a check to perform and enough time to do it well.

Elias worked until the file's sound briefly covered the street. The parcel would not return because he stopped imagining it. The next letter could wait to exist before becoming the day's principal problem.

When he set down the tool, his last note remained legible beside the drawing. He had not spent the entire afternoon asking the door who would knock.

The checked edge required no further work. Elias put down the instrument and left the piece where Marcel could retrieve it without searching among papers.

Elias looked at the space freed on the bench and resumed an ordinary job.

Marcel picked up the worked piece and checked its edge without hurry. Elias followed the verification and corrected the instrument's position when the master indicated it. Refusing to follow the carrier had earned him no promotion; he still had work from which to learn. The alley's noise stayed outside the door while they finished the check.

Elias put the finished work beside the vise. Outside, cart noise continued to cover footsteps.

* * *

Mael Vaur received the returned sample in the room above the depot. He read the delivery confirmation first, then opened the wrapping. The irregular edge remained. The piece would open no connection, and the craftsman had not made the mistake of substituting one that could.

He put a fingertip on the metal. Trace reached the residues the piece retained, and pressure returned to his perception without moving the wrapping: contact with a surface, the impact of an edge. Vaur followed the trace as far as its medium preserved it. None offered a destination for the hinge he sought. To obtain that, he would have to make a person work, not merely read an object returned to him.

Vaur turned it in the light. The irregularity formed the little point the drawing rendered poorly. Ilian had reproduced it. Helren had disputed the certification. Three people had managed to defeat a request without showing him what they kept.

The draftsman's bill was still on the table. He put it beneath the commercial reply: Ilian would return to ask for the balance. A man owed money retained a reason to reopen a conversation.

Mael took up the rejected text and crossed out the reference to a demonstration. The next question would concern something smaller. He did not need Ruy to entrust him with the entire passage at once; he needed to meet someone able to describe part of it.

He left the sample open beside the bill. He did not know the hinge's destination or reserve. Nobody had promised him access. But he had learned which signatures the workshop recognized and what work the draftsman would still carry to his door.

On the new sheet he wrote only a request for comparison. He would discuss the price after seeing who came.
