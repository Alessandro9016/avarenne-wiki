Chapter 78 — The Price of a Miracle

Yselle held the letter between two graphite-stained fingers. “This one isn't mine,” she said. Elias recognised his name and the forwarding mark from Rovenne. The pump handover copy lay separately beneath it: number one, spares, the Mutual's signature.

“He answered?”

“Apparently. I haven't opened it.”

He put it in his folder. He wanted to read it with Livia and Edith, but felt the childish temptation to hold it up to the sun. Yselle saw and shook her head. Her smile lasted until the noise from the entrance.

Ilian struck the doorframe with his shoulder. He did not fall; he clung to the wood, breathing through his mouth. His hat was gone. His folder hung from a torn strap and a dark line crossed his collar.

“Close it,” he said.

Elias moved before understanding whom he meant. The door had already begun to close when a shoe slipped into the remaining gap. The man outside pushed with both hands.

“We only want the paper.”

Yselle called to the yard workers. Ilian tried to hide his folder behind his body and lost his footing. Elias caught him beneath one arm; at that moment the door opened far enough to strike his side.

The newcomer wore a brown jacket. An old tear marked the seam beside his wrist, not enough to identify him as the school attacker. Elias saw his right hand drop towards his pocket and left Ilian against the bench.

“Out,” Yselle said. “I've already called for help.”

The man did not look at her. He watched the folder. “It doesn't belong to him.”

“Then ask the guard for it.”

The blade appeared beneath his sleeve. Elias had been thinking about knives all day; seeing one stripped away the very thought he needed. He retreated into a crate. Ilian sat down hard on the bench step with the folder between his knees.

Pump two stood behind them, its low base to Elias's left. Number three remained beneath its cloth. He invested the reference of the bench corner, something that would not move with his body. Spatial Bearing returned the true direction while he continued retreating, too quickly.

The knife did not arrive where he expected. It caught his sleeve, dragged the fabric and opened a short line across his wrist. Elias felt the sting, saw his hand still whole and was almost happy for an instant.

The man pulled Ilian's folder. The strap finally gave way. Papers fell to the floor and spread beneath the bench.

With Weight Shift, Elias took hold of the small target he could see: the blade, not the whole man. He increased its weight for a pulse. The point dropped; the thief's wrist dipped a few inches. He tightened his grip and resisted. It was enough for Yselle to empty the cleaning bucket over his chest.

The blow cost him a step. The knife struck a crate's edge. Elias did not try to repeat the exercise more forcefully; the second worker was already coming in from the yard and Ilian had begun sliding to the floor.

The key to the inner partition was on the shelf, less than two paces away. Elias reached its position, opened the small passage between that point and his palm and caught it badly: the ring struck a finger. He had to grasp it again. Small Transfer had not taught him speed.

Yselle understood which door he wanted to close. She put Ilian's sound arm over her shoulder while Elias took the other. They dragged him beyond the partition. The worker at the entrance was pushing a crate between himself and the blade.

Elias saw the thief stoop over the papers. He could have called his name, had he known it; he could have let Ilian go and tried to reach him. The draughtsman's weight shifted against him. He held on.

“Now!” Yselle shouted.

The worker backed through the partition. Elias turned the key as the knife scraped the wood outside. Blood made his fingers slip. The bolt engaged at the second attempt.

The next blow never came.

The other sounds vanished too: metal rolling off the bench, a voice in the yard, footsteps beyond the door. Elias still had his mouth open. He heard his own breathing and Ilian's, but nothing from the room they had just left.

He drew close to the wall. Near Coordinates returned the bench, the opening to the street, the space behind the crate. No plan told him who stood inside it. He could not turn a reached volume into a list of culprits.

Yselle caught his sleeve. “Don't open it.”

Elias had not heard the whole sentence, only the part beside his ear. The silence ended somewhere between them and the outer wall. That knowledge chilled his skin more than the knife.

Then a man in the street shouted for someone to be stopped and the entire shout reached him. Sound returned at once: a wheel, a curse, the bucket finishing its swing.

The worker ran to the yard exit to call for the guard again. Elias stayed with Ilian. The draughtsman held a hand against his neck; blood came from behind his ear. His eyes were open and he kept repeating that he had not gone to the depot.

“I know,” Elias said, although he did not yet.

Yselle gave him a cloth. Ilian pressed it to the wound and finally let the empty folder drop. Elias asked whether he could move his fingers, then stopped asking a doctor's questions. He simply asked what had happened.

“They were waiting outside. Said they had to recover the original. I said I was coming here.”

He inhaled too deeply. Yselle helped him sit more comfortably.

“They caught the strap. The one in the jacket. I ran. I didn't go to the depot.”

He had not defied the warning out of pride. He had closed his workplace and crossed a familiar street. Elias looked at the door they had shut and realised he could no longer use a cautious answer in the morning as a measure of safety in the afternoon.

The guard arrived before the doctor. Two men checked the entrance and the outer room; a third listened to Yselle without interrupting. Nobody promised to find the man in the jacket. Elias reported the knife, the transfer of the key, the silence. Asked whether he had seen a second assailant, he said no.

One of the men wanted to chase the thief through the side street. The guard brought him back to the entrance and asked him to show the route instead of following it alone. Elias listened to the list of corners. He had seen no face beyond the door when sound vanished; sending an ordinary man after a shadow suddenly seemed very different from wanting someone to catch the culprit.

On the working plan, he indicated the partition and the stretch where he had stopped hearing the street. He marked where he, Yselle and Ilian had been as well. No perimeter had appeared before his eyes: he had sensed only a boundary. He left the rest open. The guard sent his colleague to speak to whoever had shouted outside and asked that nobody move the bucket before the entrance had been checked.

The papers still lay scattered beneath the bench. The thief had carried some away. Not the composite document: it remained in the case Livia had left, locked in Yselle's drawer. The draughtsman had not been carrying what they wanted.

The guard had him empty the folder onto a clean table. An invoice copy had caught in the lining and the collar drawing was torn across its section. Mael Vaur's signed request was missing. Ilian named its author before everyone, this time without searching for a phrase that might save the client.

“He hasn't paid the balance. He had me carry the questions. I didn't confirm a visit.”

Yselle handed over the altered document. Livia had recorded its transfer and the guard wrote down the time he took it. Elias pointed out that other copies were already at the guard post. Ilian looked at him as though, for the first time, a copy had acquired the weight of an object capable of resistance.

The doctor found the cut behind his ear and a bruise, checked his eyes and ordered that Ilian should not spend the night alone. He bandaged Elias's wrist as well. No wound vanished. The young man felt the linen pull when he tried to turn his hand and remembered the letter in his pocket from a man who could do otherwise.

The guard kept everyone until the statements were complete and arranged a patrol past the workshop. Ilian accepted the place Yselle offered while he waited for an acquaintance. He would not return to the depot demanding explanations. Someone would accompany him to his address.

Martin arrived while they were setting the crate upright. He looked at Elias's hand and raised his own, bandaged the day before. Neither smiled. Yselle said they would close for the evening; he began helping collect the fallen metal.

Livia and Edith waited for Elias outside the workshop. They had come to the agreed contact point after failing to find him and had seen the patrol. Edith looked at his dressing, asked whether he had eaten and did not believe his answer.

First they walked to the street where shops were still open. Livia checked that nobody kept pace with them around two corners; it was no guarantee, but they did not pretend disappearing through the first door was enough. Only later did they reach Room Six's ordinary entrance.

The room still held twelve chairs. They occupied three. Elias laid Alexander's envelope on the table; dirty water had marked its edge, but the paper inside was dry.

The duke agreed to a conversation. He asked which questions they wished to begin with and announced some of his own. Elias read the lines twice. That morning they would have seemed the largest thing in the day.

“Do we tell him everything?” Edith asked.

“The assault, yes,” Livia said. “Our room, no.”

Elias nodded. They put the thirteenth on the page as a proposed day, not an appointment already made. The location would be elsewhere. They added that a commission attributed to Elias had been falsified, that Ilian had been attacked and that the authorities had received the documents. Alexander needed to be able to decide knowing what had changed.

Edith stopped them before they finished. “If he comes only because we're afraid, what happens when he isn't there?”

Elias looked at the table. The miracle had solved a real problem. It had not made the hands that worked on it untouchable.

“We aren't asking him to stand guard,” he said. “We're asking whether he recognises what we encountered. And tomorrow I'm going with Ilian to complete his statement.”

Livia wrote down the draughtsman's name. Edith noted whom they needed to warn before any visit. Nobody offered to confront an unknown Bearer. They had a copy to defend, a living witness and a silence the ordinary guard had not explained.

When they folded the letter for the next day, Elias felt the six crowns still in his pocket. His fee was intact. It would not buy an answer; that did not mean he would stop looking for one.

The Hinge retained a single opening. For a moment he thought how easy it would be to go elsewhere. He left the object where it was and returned to the table.

Edith turned over the copied drawing. Livia had made it before leaving the workshop, marking the tear in the original Ilian had retained as well. On its reverse were the words available with operator. Elias read them as he had the first time, then differently.

The component had never been the only thing they wanted to take away.
