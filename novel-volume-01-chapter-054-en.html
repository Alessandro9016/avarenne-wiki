Chapter 54 — Inside the Water

Water struck his face before he saw where the joint had gone.

Elias closed his eyes. The sound filled the room, lower near the floor and hard against metal. He tried to inhale and took water with the air. His body folded as his hand sought the rail on his right.

The reference was still there.

Not a picture of the stairs: the relationship to the point he had reached, precise even beneath wet eyelids. One short step. The step's edge remained beneath his sole, the post beyond his hand. Elias opened his fingers and found actual metal.

Noemi slipped in front of him.

He saw her shoulder change height and her hip strike the rail. She had sought the shutoff as the old joint opened; now her foot could not find the position she had counted on. Her left hand caught the bar, but her body kept turning beneath the water's force and the movement of the wet floor.

Elias reached her side. He released the rail and brought his left hand beneath her elbow. Weight passed into his arm, more alive and difficult than the dismantled liner's. Noemi tightened her fingers on the bar and stopped the movement enough not to fall.

“The key!”

He did not know whether he or she had shouted. The shelf lay ahead and a little farther over, the same place they had examined before the test. The distance had not grown. What had changed was the possibility of crossing it: moving his body to reach would remove the support he was giving Noemi.

The jet cut his vision into strips. Through the nearby geometry already reached, he sought the shelf's edge, recognized the key's small volume, and opened his right palm in the clear point beside his hip. Between object and hand was a short, connected space the curtain of water had not closed.

Small Transfer took the key from there.

Iron arrived in his palm with a precise coldness. Elias closed his fingers enough not to drop it. There had been no throw to follow, no object forced to challenge the jet to reach the right side. The hand supporting Noemi had not left her elbow.

“Here,” he said.

She moved her right hand without immediately finding his. Elias touched her wrist with the backs of his fingers, then put the key's grip into her palm. He felt her fingers close on the metal. He released it only when its weight no longer belonged to him.

The water changed its voice.

It had happened while he sought Noemi's hand: a curved surface had taken its place between the break and them, high enough to intercept the jet and bend it toward the floor. Elias saw its edge when the veil before his eyes opened. The new metal had broad supports holding it on the actual floor; its curve left the water a route along the clear side, toward the drain Noemi had had cleared.

Alexander stood near the base. The young duke did not need to cross the room to make the shelter present. His open hand lowered beside him while the already existing matter received the water's blow. The pump behind him remained motionless, its lever where Martin had left it.

Elias heard the jet against the shelter as one hears rain beneath a roof too low: the sound was very close, but the full force no longer struck his face. Spray slipped over the edge and reached his cheek. Most of the water went elsewhere, along a route that did not pass through Noemi's body.

She bent to see the shutoff.

“Hold me.”

The words gave him something to do that noise could not confuse. Elias brought his left foot closer to the step, kept his right wide enough not to cross his legs, and raised Noemi's elbow a little. He did not lift her. He gave her time to find purchase with her own body.

The wet sleeve slipped against Elias's palm. He sought a higher hold beneath the part of the arm Noemi kept bent. He felt fabric pull against the seam and eased his fingers a little, spreading support along the forearm without gripping the wrist that needed to remain free.

“Not toward you,” she said, close enough to be heard. “Hold.”

Elias stopped drawing his arm back. Noemi did not want to leave the position she had reached: she wanted to stay in it. The difference came to him as a change of weight. Her shoulder stopped seeking his, her elbow gave him a clear direction, and her body turned toward the control again.

On the curve's outer side, water made something vibrate that he could not see. The sound reached his jaw. Elias clenched his teeth and watched Noemi's right hand. The key shook no more than anything else: it was an object firmly held, ready for the point where she would bring it.

She replaced her foot where the edge was not covered by moving water. Her left hand stayed on the rail, the key in her right. To reach the control she had to turn her shoulder beyond the old bracket protruding from the pipe. Elias saw it edgewise: dark paint, a screw, and a part of the support no longer where they had found it before the test.

“The socket's underneath,” Noemi said.

He followed her gaze. The shutoff was lower than her hand, beside the pipe where the shelter did not cover it. Putting the key where the jet was and hoping would not suffice. Noemi knew the control; she sought it with a short movement, sliding the opening against the right point.

Behind them, Yselle called for people to leave.

Elias heard the attendant's name and Marcel's voice, nearer the man than the duke. The attendant had stepped toward the connection as the joint failed. Now the master took his arm and guided him along the dry side, where the shelter left room to reach the door. Gaston caught a container rolling into the passage and pushed it beneath the bench, clear of their feet.

Teren appeared in the doorway. He indicated the corridor to someone who had stopped at the threshold to look inside, then took the place left clear by the first person out. He did not try making three men pass together where one needed to turn his body.

Martin was still near the motionless lever. Yselle showed him the clear side, and he reached Marcel without touching the drive again. The duke moved half a step, letting them pass behind the base. Elias caught their movements between strips of water. Nobody stood between him and the shutoff; nobody needed him to release Noemi to show them the door.

The key struck the socket without engaging.

Noemi withdrew it a little. Elias saw her mouth move but could not hear the words. The jet kept striking the curve, and the noise hid even the brief knock of iron against iron. She changed the angle, guided the opening with her thumb, and lowered the key until she found full contact.

“Got it,” she said.

Elias felt the elbow press against his palm. He supported the new direction instead of pulling her toward him. The rail remained the reference his body returned to when his sight tried to make him retreat. Moving his right foot a little kept his knee from twisting beneath the load.

Noemi began turning.

The control offered a brief resistance. She leaned her shoulder's weight into the key, using the narrow space between shelter and pipe. The old bracket caught her sleeve. Fabric pulled, then opened against the broken edge as she completed the movement toward the shutoff.

Elias saw a red thread appear on her right forearm and vanish beneath the water.

Noemi's grip did not open. Her left hand still held the bar; her right had brought the control far enough to change the sound beyond the shelter. It was not finished. Return water continued, but the pressure against the curved metal hesitated in a way the young man felt through the floor too.

“Again,” she said.

Elias did not know whether she meant the valve or herself. He strengthened his support beneath her left elbow, on the side away from the wound. Blood appeared again lower down, drawn toward her wrist. The wound lay where the old iron had reached; the shelter had not shut it away from the water enough to show all of it.

Noemi sought room for another fraction of a turn.

The shelter's edge prevented her from bringing the elbow farther forward but did not cover the control. She turned her wrist instead of her shoulder. Elias followed with his support, keeping his own hip toward the rail. The key remained engaged. A low sound ran through the pipe, different from the knock that had opened it.

Water on the floor found the drain. Elias saw a small object turning near the curve's edge, caught and released by the current. Beneath his sole the surface remained slippery; he eased weight from that foot enough to seek the rough part of the step. He did not fall. The reference had not dried the floor, but it had let him know where he was putting his weight.

His right hand was empty. For an instant his fingers sought the key; then he saw it in Noemi's grip, where it needed to be. The small exercise was over; she was still doing the rest with her own arm and the support he could give.

Alexander looked toward them beyond the shelter's edge. Elias could see him only from the chest up. The duke followed the key's movement, then turned toward the route to the drain. The curve stayed firm on its supports even when his hand moved to show Gaston an object in the passage.

Teren was guiding the last man back from the doorway toward the corridor. Yselle's voice came more clearly for an instant, calling that the pump was stopped. Elias could see its intact body a short distance away; water continued coming from the old section beyond the connection, against the shelter.

For a moment the woman took her own weight on the higher foot. Elias felt the load in his arm ease and used the little space to straighten his elbow. He had not left his place beside her. His other fingers brushed his jacket, empty and open, while return water continued striking the metal.

Beneath the light dirtied by water, he saw the mark the key had followed on the socket. He could not read all of it, but Noemi could: her wrist changed angle in the final stretch, and her shoulder lowered toward the support he maintained. Elias readied his arm for the weight returning.

Noemi brought the control to the end of its travel.

Elias felt her elbow cease pushing and supported the weight returning toward him. The key remained in her right hand. The blow against the curved wall weakened; water stopped seeking its upper edge and began falling closer to the floor.

The valve was closed.
