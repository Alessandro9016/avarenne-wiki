Chapter 21 — A Small Key

The key ceased to be on the bench and appeared on the nearby shelf.

Edith reached out at once, then stopped before touching it. She had seen no throw. The metal had not crossed the space in the way her body continued to expect.

“May I take it?”

Elias nodded.

The key was as cold as before. Edith checked both sides, almost as though she had been handed an imitation prepared for a performance. Elias knew that expression. The first time, he had worn it before his own hands.

“Is it a workshop key?”

“For a small box. Marcel knows we're using it.”

“Good. I'd have been sorry to discover afterward that I'd touched some fundamental secret.”

Livia moved a chair for her. Afternoon light slanted through the window, exposing the part of the floor where Elias had abandoned a ruler the day before. He picked it up before anyone tripped: not every workshop hazard deserved an Effigy.

Edith had come to agree on her contribution for the twenty-third. Before beginning, she had asked Elias what he could actually do. She did not want to discover it while an injured person waited to be moved.

“I can transfer this,” he said, indicating the key. “Not you.”

“And if you make a person lighter?”

“They don't stop having a spine.”

Edith looked at him with fresh interest.

“Can I have you repeat that tomorrow?”

He smiled.

He had not performed the transfer to demonstrate a rank he did not possess. The two points were nearby, reached, and connected; the object was small. Even so, he felt the exercise's cost behind his eyes. He took water and did not offer a second attempt merely to see surprise on the nurse's face again.

Edith did not put the key back on the bench immediately. She held it on her open palm, studying the point from which it had vanished. Elias awaited acknowledgement of the power and realized from her next question that the nurse was thinking of something else.

“What if I move while you do it?”

“I didn't take your hand as the destination. The point was the shelf.”

She nodded. She was not seeking a way to make things difficult; she wanted to imagine the situation without remaining still in the one position that would let him succeed.

“And if someone needs the object you're taking away?”

Elias looked at the key. It would have been easy to speak of impossible thefts, locks, things glimpsed for an instant. His ability's conditions were not those of every adventure someone could imagine on hearing the word transfer.

“I have to reach what I exercise the ability on. Wanting something whose location I don't know isn't enough.”

Edith repeated the gesture with which she had withdrawn her fingers. She did it slowly, then left her hand on the bench, well away from the shelf.

“So I shouldn't expect you to put a tool in my hand while I think it's coming from somewhere else.”

“You can expect me to warn you.”

She smiled. It was the first question whose useful answer required no explanation of a magical limit.

Elias realized his mind kept seeking a second, more convincing demonstration. He had acknowledged the cost and drunk water, yet others' surprise remained a pleasure difficult to abandon. He stayed seated. His spiritual reserve was not the only thing he would consume if every conversation became a request for admiration.

Marcel passed through the workshop and asked whether the key had finished traveling. Edith raised it. He indicated where to leave it afterward; he did not make it so precious an object that their discussion must stop.

For Elias, that naturalness remained harder to learn than the transfer. The ability had entered his life as an immense promise, but a key could have been moved without deserving the entire morning.

Livia had brought a sheet with three requests. The first concerned what Edith could say about Etienne's recovery. The second proposed a brief lesson on helping an injured person somewhere without a doctor. The third was Elias's, added at the bottom in quicker handwriting: establish whether the apparatus could be useful for an evacuation.

Edith read everything and placed a finger on the third line.

“What do you already know?”

Elias described the two points, the distance within the same lot, the limited duration. He did not use the word salvation to sell a passage not yet tested for the purpose.

“And if someone can't stand?”

“We won't test it with an injured person.”

“That was the condition I was about to ask for.”

Livia added a note in the margin, then stopped her pen.

“Writing it down isn't enough. If something changes tomorrow, we stop.”

No patient had been invited. The planned weight was an inert, measured load. Marcel would direct the check, keeping the ordinary exit available.

Edith put the key down before Elias.

“For the first request, I want to speak to Etienne before the meeting. I can't bring anything he hasn't authorized me to show.”

“Even if it would help us?”

The question escaped before Elias could make it less revealing.

“Especially then.”

Livia drew a line beneath the request, not through Edith's name. They were not erasing a person for refusing a service.

Edith returned to the first request and had Elias describe what he wanted from the account. He began with the measurements, the bench's position, and the error attributed to preparation. He stopped on realizing she had witnessed none of those three things.

“I want nobody to be able to say we're hiding the injury.”

“I'm not hiding it.”

“I know. I meant…”

She waited. Elias wanted Livia to intervene with a better sentence, but the copyist gave him time to distinguish his need from the service he requested.

“I'm afraid it'll look as though the recovery is convenient for me. That I use it only when it suits me.”

Edith leaned against the chair's back. She did not call the fear absurd or particularly noble.

“Then when we speak to him, we let him decide what information to make available. That doesn't establish who is right about the bench.”

Elias nodded. He had found someone ready to do something for the dispute, but not to become an answer to his fear of resembling whoever had prepared the first offer.

Livia changed the request's heading. She put the concrete task first, leaving aside the reason they wanted it. The page became simpler to read. Elias could have shown it to Etienne without also burdening him with understanding all his reasons.

Edith checked the time. She had to remember her shift even while enjoying herself here. Elias saw the movement and stopped adding a new question every time the previous one was answered. He wanted her to return because the meeting had been useful, rather than discover too late that she had lost part of her day.

The nurse rested her notebook on the table. The cover's edge was worn from being pushed into a pocket too narrow for it. She showed Livia a page without clinical information: the ink had spread where water had reached it.

“Could you have prevented that?”

“For a short interval, with access to the surface. Not on all your notebooks forever.”

Livia took a scrap, wrote a few words, and invested its surface with power. She held it against the damp edge of a cloth. The letters retained their boundaries where water should have spread them through the paper.

When she removed the sheet and let the exercise end, Edith examined it without easy enthusiasm.

“During a long shift I might need it while you're doing something else.”

“Yes.”

“Then I still need a better cover.”

Elias laughed. Livia handed her the scrap, amused too.

For a few moments none of them spoke about the meeting. Edith described a uniform with pockets so tight she had to carry her scissors where she should never have needed to search for them. Livia compared that cruelty with a dress that would not let her sit down without losing a pencil. Elias concluded he had never properly valued his waistcoat.

The protected scrap passed from hand to hand. Elias watched it tilt toward the window; it was the same writing he had seen appear beneath Livia's pen, still legible where damp should have broken it apart.

Edith asked to hold it beside her notebook's cover without wetting the pages. The copyist allowed it after reminding her that the exercise had ended. The nurse chiefly compared dimensions: she wanted to understand how much ordinary surface a protection would need to cover for her to carry it even without a Bearer beside her.

“I bought it too small,” she said of the notebook. “To fit in my pocket.”

“And now the pocket ruins the notebook,” Elias observed.

“How perceptive. I'll gladly entrust you with the next inventory of my poor decisions.”

Livia laughed and removed a ruler before Elias could actually try measuring the cover. They found themselves discussing where things belonged on clothes and how often their designers imagined hands that were always free.

Edith tried slipping the notebook into her pocket. It caught at the edge; she jerked it out and set it before Marcel. “A cover that survives this. Without calling a Bearer whenever it rains.” Livia turned the notebook to inspect its spine.

It was Edith who returned the conversation to the key.

“Sometimes I think I'd like to learn Heart.”

She did not say it as a question addressed to a recruiter. She sounded almost annoyed by how easily the desire took the shape of a solution.

Elias waited.

“Then someone tells me I'd heal more people, and I get angry. As though every ability I might acquire already belonged to whoever would need it.”

Livia did not tell her she should therefore abandon the idea.

“You aren't coming tomorrow to promise that,” she said.

Edith nodded and closed the notebook.

Marcel came looking for the key before closing time. Edith showed it to him, and he indicated the small box it belonged to. The nurse put the metal into the lock, turned it, and watched the lid rise just as it would have a moment before the transfer.

“I wanted to check it could still do this,” she said.

Elias laughed. He had not considered the demonstration from the perspective of someone who wanted a usable object back, not merely an impossibility.

Marcel closed the box and left the key on her palm again so she could compare it with the point from which it had vanished. They activated nothing. The supernatural demonstration was already over; now Edith was trying to bring her ordinary perceptions together.

Edith put down the key and withdrew her fingers once more. “Now I expect it to vanish even if you aren't looking.”

“It won't.”

“I know.” She picked it up and put it where Marcel had indicated. “My hand hasn't understood yet.”

Elias looked at his own fingers. For him the stretch between bench and shelf had been an accessible route, a small target, a recognizable effort. Edith had seen the world skip a step. He smiled before finding a better explanation.

They agreed on the time, the contact, and the staggered arrival. When she left, Elias returned the key to Marcel and removed from the program the idea of a demonstration with someone needing rescue.

The test would show a passage. It would have no right to pretend it was already a treatment.
