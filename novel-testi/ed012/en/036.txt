Elias did not find a crowd waiting for him.

The morning of May thirty-first offered a corridor, some occupied chairs, and someone asking him not to stop in front of the door. The city had problems other than his, and none had suspended itself to make room for him.

Livia arrived with the authorized copies. Marcel brought his own examination. Gaston kept the series separate, as though a folder too narrow might still try to recount them beneath the same position.

Etienne Brac greeted everyone and sat where directed. Edith stayed near him until asked to clarify the extract's origin. She was not there to certify every word the worker spoke.

Vern arrived before they were called.

He had the corrected sheet. Elias saw it and felt no relief until the man submitted it among the records for examination.

A man came to ask who was present for the examination. Gaston indicated the names, then showed where he kept the document references. He did not try to put the folder in the hands of anyone wearing office clothes.

Elias realized he knew the pages' order better than the order in which they must enter. There was nothing unusual about that difference; nevertheless, it still made him afraid that he had already arrived without being properly prepared.

Livia asked whether he wanted to review a specific line. Elias looked at the case and knew that opening it would end in checking everything.

“No. I've read them.”

The copyist nodded. She did not ask him to repeat what he had just said as a way of demonstrating that he had sincerely meant it. He had what he needed, and doubt that would remain after a second reading was not necessarily proof of a lack.

Vern submitted his account and returned to the wall. Etienne greeted him without approaching. Elias sought peace or hostility in the gesture, then stopped: the worker had come to describe his work and obtain what was owed, not to supply a concluding scene to the boy's story.

When called, Elias rose before the voice finished and had to wait for those ahead. He forced himself not to enter with the folder already open. The room was not the warehouse. The people whose responsibility it was to listen to them would be entitled to ask about one thing at a time.

Inside, the room seemed unsuited to the grandeur Elias had given the day during the preceding nights. The table was wide enough for the documents. The people appointed to listen had other sheets beside his.

They asked his name and the work performed.

“Elias Veyne. Preliminary measurements on the portable bench in the indicated position before the test on April twenty-first.”

His voice came out drier than he wanted. That was no reason to repeat everything from the beginning.

He explained where he had worked and which references he had used. Asked whether he had witnessed the relocation, he said no.

“How, then, can you state it was moved?”

Elias felt the old urge to defend himself against a question before hearing it to the end.

He identified what he knew directly and what came from subsequent comparisons. He did not claim Marcel's inspection as his own. He did not turn Livia's copy into an original order no one had retained.

The representative for the commission asked whether the accuracy of the first series sufficed to demonstrate the safety of the whole operation.

“No,” Elias said.

For an instant he felt he had opened the most vulnerable point of his own defense.

“It says which relationship was measured. It doesn't authorize using it after changing that relationship.”

Someone at the table asked him to indicate on the plan the point from which the reading was taken. Elias approached close enough to see the line without moving other documents. His hand suddenly seemed too large for the page.

“Does what you know now also come from the later inspection?”

“Yes. I can distinguish the two.”

They asked him to. He began with what he had measured before the test. At the second position, he named Marcel's comparison and the references he had followed. He claimed no continuous perception of the bench between days.

The commissioning party's representative returned to the first certification. The measurement was correct, he observed, but the sheet did not describe every operational step. Elias felt an urgent need to say he could not be accused of that. He stopped and answered the question: that certification did not guarantee any subsequent use, precisely because it concerned a defined arrangement.

“From which reference?” they asked again. Elias returned to the plan. This time he left aside the inspection's account and put his finger on the point. The person at the table marked it beside the value, then had him indicate the bench's later position. The two notes remained separate.

When he finished, they showed him where to sit. He returned feeling he had left something exposed. It was testimony's limit: he could state what he had done and where the rest came from. He could not make a measurement truer by offering knowledge he had not possessed.

He put his hands on his knees and heard his master's name. For the first time, his own body did not have to carry the entire explanation into the room.

Marcel was called about the comparison. He described the geometric reconstructions and what they had not done: no reactivation of the field with people exposed, no magical dating of every hole in the floor.

Gaston showed the second series. Asked what moment it referred to, he answered without seeking a new way to say it.

“The bench already moved.”

They had him distinguish what he had measured from what he had been told. The distinction did not destroy his work. It made it checkable by people who had not been in the warehouse.

Livia confirmed the comparison of the authentic copy with the first documented position. She did not say Archive let her know the guilt of a man her exercise had never reached.

As she spoke, Elias realized he was no longer counting how often his name was mentioned. He was following the passage from one part of the evidence to the next.

The representative of the commission took the copy of the second series and set it beside the first, covering the headings.

“From here, the margins don’t look incompatible.”

Gaston reached out without taking the sheet. “May I see the position?”

The man lifted the page. The reference to the relocated bench became visible again. Gaston read it, then repeated the reference in the first series.

The representative searched for the point on the plan. He was no longer dealing with an isolated number.

“Can you authenticate responsibility through this copy?” he asked Livia.

“No. I authenticated the comparison I made.”

“Then that mark doesn’t decide who ordered the move.”

“No.”

The man returned the sheet to the clerk recording the proceedings. Elias looked at Vern, still waiting. The gap magic could not fill had his name on it.

Then it was Vern's turn.

The lot supervisor sat, rested his hands on the table, and confirmed the corrected account. He said he had authorized the move to recover the working day. He said he knew a new calibration was needed and had relied on the stated margin without having it performed.

Elias had waited for Vern to look at him. Instead the man watched the person taking notes. Whenever a sentence was repeated, he confirmed its contents.

Elias felt his hand seek the folder. He left it on his knees. The words he had pursued outside this room were reaching the office without his having to speak them for the other man.

“Did the decision about the margin also concern the supplier’s instructions?” the representative of the commission asked.

Vern described the instructions he remembered. Elias saw the clerk recording the proceedings look for the supplier’s reference. For an instant he feared the interview might begin all over again: another name, another sheet, an explanation long enough to make the foreman’s choice disappear.

“Did those instructions require the original position to be maintained?”

Vern looked at the plan.

“The first calibration referred to it.”

“Were you told that it replaced a check after relocation?”

The man kept his fingers against the edge of the table for a moment. “That isn’t what I’m saying.”

The clerk had him confirm the distinction. Elias stopped pressing his heels against the chair legs. The supplier’s instructions remained among the records; Vern’s decision remained where he had made it too.

Elias looked at Etienne, who did not return his gaze. The worker was listening to the man who had given the order.

When he was called, his arm moved normally as he set his cap beside the chair.

Etienne put both hands on the table. Elias recognized the gesture with which he steadied wood beneath a hammer; this time there was nothing to hold still. The worker described which passage he had come through on the day of the test. When he searched for the bench’s position on the plan, the clerk recording the proceedings had him turn the sheet toward himself.

They asked whether he remembered the bench's exact position while working. Etienne indicated its relationship to the passage without assigning it a measurement seen only on later plans.

“Did you attend the checks after the accident?”

“Yes.”

“How do you distinguish what you remember from what you learned there?”

The worker took a moment. Elias felt an impulse to intervene to prevent him seeming uncertain. He stayed seated. Etienne had voiced that very fear the previous evening and had not asked Elias to spare him every question.

The man described the working gesture he remembered and stopped where the number obtained through comparison began. He did not make pain seem an ability capable of recording every distance. His listeners could return to the measurements without attributing them to him.

Edith clarified the extract's origin when called. She described her observations and the authorized use's limits. She did not surrender a whole archive to prove she concealed nothing. Livia let authentication concern the actual comparison, not every statement a listener might desire.

Elias saw the two working without confusing them into one voice come to save him. Edith knew treatment, Livia attestation. Evidence requiring several contributions was no less legible; it prevented one sentence pretending to contain everything.

When discussion returned to recovery, Etienne moved his cap to put his hand on the table. He used it normally. Elias watched and understood the answer need not make the arm less healthy to keep the injury present.

A question about the assistance received made him turn his head slightly toward Edith.

“I've recovered,” he said. “That doesn't erase the days I couldn't work.”

The person taking notes asked for the sentence again. Etienne repeated it. The commissioning representative lowered his eyes to the listed days; Edith put the copy among the pages shown.

Elias waited for a question directed at him. Nobody asked one.

Then they were invited to wait outside. He rose with the folder closed: measurement, signature, and healed arm had occupied the same table for a moment. The outcome had not, yet.
