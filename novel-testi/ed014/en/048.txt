Elias arrived with the toolbox in his right hand and his prepared greeting in the wrong part of his mouth.

The duke stood beside the bench. He had left his coat at the entrance; window light caught his black hair and settled in his golden eyes. Elias had imagined a seated man, surrounded by enough space for anyone entering to know immediately where to stop. Instead Alexander Valcienne was about to move a chair out of the way.

Marcel greeted him with his title. Elias followed without stumbling, then realized he was still gripping the toolbox. Martin showed him a clear corner of the bench. The young man put it down; its clasp clicked too loudly in the silence he had constructed around his own entrance.

Yselle already held the drawing.

“Veyne,” she said, introducing him. “The dimensions are his. You can ask him about the liner too.”

The duke looked at Elias. He did not smile to reassure him in advance, and the young man preferred that as yet undecided attention to a kindness he would have to earn without knowing how.

“Does this passage remain accessible with the machine assembled?”

Alexander indicated the enlarged detail. Elias approached closely enough to see the exact point and forgot the clasp.

“With the change to the edge, yes. We passed the tool through with the head in this position. Here you can see the catch's notch.”

He looked up. The duke had not looked elsewhere. Elias took the sample lifting tool left beside the prototype and put it in the position shown. The head was still closed; the usable route remained visible from outside.

“To replace the liner, the operator opens it here. This catch isn't the drive catch. The first sheet didn't make that clear.”

“It took a man of ingenuity to demonstrate it,” Martin said.

“It took a man who didn't read everything in front of him,” Yselle replied.

Alexander looked at his aunt's husband, then at the drawing. The brief smile changed his face enough to remind Elias that this was also a nephew visiting. It did not remove his height, his name, or the way the room made space for him.

“Have you tried reassembling it from these instructions?”

“The day before yesterday,” Elias said. “With one more notch to add. And a cloth to replace.”

He showed the corner where they had drawn the supports. The duke asked about the cloth. Martin described the fiber beneath the gasket without making the account a defense of his own work. Alexander bent over the head's edge; he did not touch it until Yselle showed him where it might be dirty.

Elias suddenly wanted to show him the machine running. Until that morning he had imagined every question from the duke extending the wait for judgment. Now he wanted the sound of the stroke to give the words a body.

Yselle called Gaston for the water. The space around the base had been cleared; the containers were those used in the test, large enough that nobody would need to run at the first startup. Teren remained beside the side door, with one guard visible beyond it. Alexander briefly signaled him when he moved from the drawing to the prototype. The head of security merely changed his vantage point.

The young duke indicated the tank on the site drawing.

“Is that the height you need to reach?”

Elias answered before thinking about the title. He gave the figure and showed the reference used in the Mutual's courtyard. Gaston added the water's ordinary route; Yselle let both finish before pointing to the machine's intended outlet.

“Does the old section pass there?” Alexander asked.

“The drawn route is the one surveyed,” Elias said. “For the covered stretch, we have the available references. We haven't opened every joint.”

The duke studied the difference between the pipe's line and the base's outline. He asked where the operator could stand without blocking the transport of containers. Elias showed him the passage he had seen occupied by buckets. He did not describe the visit as though he had discovered a hidden city: a low door, a staircase, two people who had to wait for someone else to pass before doing their own work.

Alexander moved the chair back half a step. The route between bench and prototype opened. Then he looked at Yselle.

“Now?”

She nodded. Martin checked the container was in place and began the test. The first sound was more modest than Elias would have wished for a duke: a dry stroke, a regular knock, the support wood answering faintly. Then water reached the outlet and fell into the container with a full sound.

Alexander followed the lever's movement. He did not merely watch the stream. He looked at the linkage, the guide, and the place where a hand would need to exert force. Elias realized he was following the duke's gaze instead of the pump and made himself return to the head's edge. It was dry.

Gaston asked a question about output that the young man could not resolve from the interval he had just observed. Marcel passed him the time reference. Elias noted volume and duration; the answer came from their relationship, not the impression that there seemed to be plenty of water.

Yselle compared it with the previous test. The value was consistent with what they expected from the prototype. The duke listened, asked about a return flow, and had them show him the relevant part of the drawing.

“If water remains above the pump when it stops?”

Yselle explained the intended behavior and the separation from the old circuit. Martin stopped operating the drive. The stream changed, thinned, and left a tail of drops. Elias looked at the container and thought of the time Noemi spent filling it by hand.

Alexander waited for the sound to end before asking to see the liner.

They opened the head with the right tools. Martin took the time needed to release the catch, lifted the component with Elias, and rested it on the wooden strips. The duke removed a glove. Yselle gave him a cloth, then the component.

The cylinder passed into his hands without visible effort. Elias remembered his own straining arm two days earlier. He could not decide whether the duke was stronger than he appeared or whether he himself had held the component in the worst possible way. Alexander's attention was already on its inner surface.

“Is this the corrected one?”

“Yes,” Yselle said. “The troublesome sample is there.”

Elias passed her the rough component. On the bench the two cylinders looked almost identical until light entered them. Alexander leaned closer, changed his angle, and asked what machining had produced the difference. Yselle answered precisely enough to force a second question.

Marcel joined the explanation when they reached the control measurement. His voice was not the deferential one of his greeting; it was the voice he used with a customer capable of understanding why something was taking time. The duke did not seem offended by the change.

Elias opened the toolbox and took out the caliper. He showed where it could enter and the part that needed a different reference. Yselle indicated the faulty liner. “He found the first narrowing, using Astrolabe.” Alexander asked whether his Effigy let him find it before dismantling.

“If I can reach the volume, I can compare its geometry. I don't get the alloy, or how it will wear, from that distance.”

“And someone without your Effigy?”

“They can check the dimensions here. That's why the drawing has the section and the access for instruments.”

The duke studied the drawing again. “So the measurement stays useful when you leave.”

Elias nodded. It had never seemed a small thing to him; hearing it asked that way made him realize how often he had presented it as a detail of his own exercise.

Yselle took back the cylinder and set it beside the faulty liner. Alexander returned his attention to the three empty spaces. They had been cleared for the intended machines, with supports prepared and room to move between one body and another. Their order made the absence more visible.

Beside the first support, a small board bore a dark burn mark. Alexander brushed it and looked at his aunt.

“This bench wasn't in the house.”

“I found it when I took the premises. The one you remember was too short.”

Martin indicated the joint beneath the surface. “So was this one. Then she decided she could lengthen it.”

Yselle showed where the addition had been supported to keep the top from flexing. The duke bent to look underneath; Elias had to move the toolbox blocking his view. For a few moments they discussed the old wood with a familiarity that included neither him nor Gaston. The young man felt outside the conversation and, at the same instant, less watched.

When Alexander straightened, dust marked his knee. Yselle pointed it out with the same pencil she had used to correct the drawing. He brushed the fabric and returned to the empty spaces. Elias saw the attention on his face change; the man who remembered the family bench was again seeking the missing part of the work before him.

“How long will it take to obtain the others?” he asked.

His aunt did not answer with a single figure. She showed him the material list, the difficult operations, and those for which they might find another workshop. Some parts could be obtained; others kept the entire machine in the form of a list. Elias watched the engineer's finger stop at the line he had already seen marked red.

“And if only these arrive?” Alexander asked.

Yselle produced the smaller drawing prepared with Martin. She did not present it as the real project they had hidden. She set it beside the other drawing and showed what the service would lose. Martin approached the locking ring, where they had needed to leave room for a wrench.

The duke studied both drawings for a long time. Elias had time to see he was not looking for somewhere to sign. He followed the components that reappeared unchanged and those altered to remain accessible. Then he returned to the still open prototype.

“Which of these items would a complete machine change?”

Yselle raised her head. “A purchase already available?”

“A machine in this configuration. Already whole.”

Marcel rested a hand on the bench's edge. Gaston looked at the prototype as though it might have concealed a second outlet. Elias felt a question form and stop: did the duke intend to bring other pumps from some place they knew nothing about?

Yselle did not look toward the door.

“How whole?”

“Assembled. With the moving parts and surfaces you've defined. And the spare parts, if you show me which.”

Elias lowered his eyes to the caliper still open in his hand. It was the instrument with which he had measured the problem. It offered no help in understanding the scale of that sentence. He closed it before setting it down; the small gesture let him remain where he needed to listen.

“Can you do this?” Yselle asked.

Alexander looked at her. “I want to understand exactly what I'm offering before trying it here.”

His aunt drew the sheet toward herself, far enough to include the prototype and the supports in one view. Elias waited for an instruction to leave. Nobody gave it. Martin remained beside the corrected component; Marcel lifted his finger from the material line he had followed until then.

“Then tell me what you want in exchange,” Yselle said.

Alexander did not look for the chair. “Let's begin with that.”

Elias stayed with his hand on the open toolbox. The work he had brought had reached the drawing. Now, between the same windows, the question had taken a form nobody had taught him to measure.
