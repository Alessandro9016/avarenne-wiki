The number one passed through the doorway a finger's width at a time.

Elias saw it appear between the jamb and the transporter's shoulder. The pump advanced on its prepared supports, a man on either side and enough wood beneath the weight to keep the metal from striking the threshold directly. The cart remained in the courtyard. From the porters' breathing, witnessing a miracle, or merely hearing about one, did not seem to make a machine lighter.

“A little toward me,” the man on the left said.

Elias indicated the space behind his heel. “There's the edge.”

The man found it with his sole and changed his footing. Gaston held the door fully open. Noemi had moved two containers and a basket that had occupied the service room access on the survey day; one stubborn corner remained, too narrow for transport and wide enough for those who passed through every day.

The morning of June sixth had begun with that problem, rather than the stream of water Elias had imagined on his way home the previous evening.

The pump crossed the threshold. Martin guided the frame's side toward its support, then removed his fingers and let the men lower the weight. The white number faced the entrance. For the first time the machine had a place other than the one where it had been born.

Noemi looked at the cleared passage. “Does the door close now?”

Gaston moved it slowly. The leaf passed without touching the frame and came to rest against the wall again. The woman nodded and took the basket outside.

Elias followed her with his eyes. People in the courtyard waited to resume small tasks already begun: a strap to adjust, a tool to wash, a parcel that could not stay damp. The Mutual had not closed for the day because a pump had arrived at the heart of its service. It had merely needed to make room for the test.

Marcel called the young man. The base needed to remain legible against the references, and the final position did not coincide with the edge along which the transporter wanted to slide it. Elias reached the nearby distances, compared them with the drawing, and indicated the correct point. The man looked first at him, then at Yselle. Marcel confirmed the reference without changing the young man's words.

They shifted the support. The wood made a low sound. Elias wrote down the position reached while Martin checked he had room for the lever's stroke.

Alexander arrived with Yselle from the other side of the courtyard. Teren stopped at the corridor entrance and examined the room before choosing where to stand. The two guards did not both station themselves in front of the door. Elias saw one keeping clear the stretch of passage they had just used.

Noemi showed the duke the wet patch on the floor.

“That's slippery without a new machine.”

Alexander changed his route. “Thank you.”

“Don't thank me yet. I have a whole place to show you.”

The young duke smiled, but looked toward the connection immediately afterward. The pump remained stopped; its metal showed no change from the previous day's tests. Elias felt a sudden pleasure at recognizing his handprint, almost erased beneath the cloth Martin had used.

The connection required hands and tools. The attendant held the prepared section while Martin brought the connector closer; Marcel checked the fit before they tightened it. Elias passed the wrench, then took up the drawing to leave the bench clear. When they removed the temporary support, the weight remained on the base and the points prepared to receive it. Yselle watched the transition and had them check that the drive could still complete its stroke without striking the edge.

The Mutual's attendant explained the sections he knew. Service water entered from the indicated side, the prepared section led to the rise toward the tank, and the old shutoff isolated the existing circuit. Yselle had him show the point. She did not settle for a gesture toward the wall: she wanted to see where the new connection ended and what they had found there began.

Elias traced the same division on the drawing. They had measured accessible references and opened the places needed for connection. The pipe could not all be read from the room: it continued behind surfaces, with a history not fully represented in the drawing.

“This section first,” Yselle said. “Then we see how the rise responds.”

The attendant nodded. He was close enough to the work to ask what they meant by response; Marcel indicated where they would observe return flow and sealing during the test. The man then showed an old stain on the wall. He said it predated his shift without giving it a date he did not know.

Elias marked it in the margin. The stain ended before the visible joint; he followed its pale edge without finding fresh water. It was part of what they could see, beside other things they still had to examine.

Noemi approached the old shutoff. The control lay beyond a pipe joint, beside the three steps leading to the walkway. The handrail separated the rising edge from the working area. It was not handsome: hands and damp had worn the finish, leaving a smoother surface in the most frequently used places.

“The key is still here,” she said.

On the nearby shelf lay the short key she had shown him during the survey. Noemi lifted it, displayed its opening, and replaced it with the grip facing anyone approaching from the pump. No cloth covered it.

“Closing this stops the return from the old section,” the attendant said.

Yselle had him trace the route again with his hand. The key remained on the shelf, a little beyond the first handrail post. Elias saw its position in relation to his shoulder, the edge, and the shutoff's socket. The distances were short, concrete enough not to preserve merely in a sentence.

He rested his hand on the rail. The metal was cold; the post did not move when he transferred a little weight to it. He reached that point with Spatial Bearing and maintained the reference. The relationship to the edge remained available even when he turned his head toward the bench.

Gaston followed the gesture. “The handrail?”

“Yes. If there's a splash, I want to find the passage without groping for it.”

“The one going up or coming down?”

Elias looked at the steps. “Both, if I don't stand in it.”

The other man smiled and moved toward the dry side with the notes. Elias released the handrail, took a step, and touched it again. The reference preserved the relationship to his movement; it had not changed the surface beneath his hand.

They moved unnecessary containers away for the test. Martin kept the collecting vessel accessible without blocking the exit. The attendant remained where he could see the connection; Noemi stood near the old shutoff, where she knew the route to the corridor. Alexander was beside the base with the pipe in view. Yselle made sure she could speak to whoever moved the lever without shouting over her own shoulder.

Elias sought the time reference. The morning did not yet seem destined for a particular memory. A man coughed in the courtyard, an inner door closed, and Noemi nudged aside something small that would trip the first distracted person.

A worker appeared from the courtyard with a hinge in her hand. The leather had been removed from the metal, and a little screw remained in its housing, too dirty to reassemble. She looked at the shifted containers and asked Noemi where she could wash it.

“In the low basin. Leave the other water here for the test.”

“I need a cloth too.”

Noemi indicated one without leaving the shutoff. The worker passed along the clear side, took what she needed, and went out again. Elias saw her sit with the component on her knees. A bandage left her thumbnail exposed; she used the other hand to hold the screw without dropping it between the stones.

“When it reaches the top, we'll use it for this too,” Noemi said, seeing him watch.

He followed the tank's height. Until then he had chiefly thought about the buckets that would stop climbing. Now he saw the work below: one hand occupied holding a component, one washing it, the need for water that arrived without demanding a third person.

Gaston called the first volume reference. Elias returned to the page and wrote it beside the space for time. The worker no longer watched the pump. The screw had begun turning, and she bent over the metal, too close to success to raise her head.

Noemi told her to call if the hinge still had play. Then she turned toward Yselle, ready to follow the test.

“Ready?” Yselle asked.

Answers came from different places. The first stroke was short.

Water entered the prepared section. Elias followed the sound, hollow at first, then fuller as the circuit took what they gave it. The pump's head stayed dry. Martin regulated the movement according to the engineer's signal. Gaston called the first reading, and the young man wrote it down.

The test advanced without demanding the full stroke immediately. The attendant watched the new connection, scanned the old pipe, and confirmed he saw no leaks. Yselle had him wait another interval. Water rose toward the tank, changing the sound in the section reached. Elias looked in the direction he knew from the survey.

Noemi saw him looking up. “It isn't full.”

“I'm waiting for the mark.”

“You'll see it. Carrying buckets, it seems much closer.”

He smiled. The engineer called a pause, and the lever stopped. They listened to the circuit settle. The pump did not move on its own to finish the interrupted work; the return through the old section made a separate sound, which the attendant recognized as he indicated the rise.

They resumed after checking. Elias shifted half a step to avoid blocking Gaston's reading. The handrail stayed on his right, the key ahead and a little farther over on the shelf. Noemi was closer to the valve than anyone else, with room to turn.

The first unusual sound was not loud enough to fill the room.

It resembled a knock inside the pipe, followed by a brief vibration. Elias looked at the pump, found its movement regular, and sought the connection. Yselle had already turned toward the old pipe.

“Stop.”

Martin completed the part of the stroke beneath his hand and left the lever still. The pump stopped. Elias saw the drive motionless and still heard water moving beyond the connection. The tank and the full section had not ceased weighing on the circuit.

“The return,” Yselle said. “Look there.”

The attendant changed position to see better. Noemi turned toward the shutoff. The young man heard a second knock, lower than the first. A bright thread appeared on the existing joint, absent a moment earlier.

“Don't go closer,” Marcel told the man.

Elias raised a hand without knowing whom to show it to. The key was on the shelf. His handrail reference stayed precise as the light on the joint became water.

The old union's edge moved.

Not a tremor in the new body, not a part Martin could stop by gripping the lever: the pipe's mouth began separating from its support. Elias saw pale metal appear in the gap and Noemi shift her weight to reach the socket.

The joint gave way with a sound that had no time to remain distinct from the jet.
