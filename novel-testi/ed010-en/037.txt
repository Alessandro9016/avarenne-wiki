They were made to wait outside.

Elias rose feeling he had left something in the room: not a sheet, but the part of himself that had spent weeks answering imaginary questions.

In the corridor Marcel offered water. The glass was ordinary, without a property that might make him more capable of reaching the end of the day.

“Did I talk too much?” Elias asked.

“You finished your answers.”

“That isn't the same thing.”

“I know.”

His mentor sat. He did not offer a prediction in the form of certainty.

Etienne and Edith spoke nearby. Gaston checked that he still had his notebook, then stopped checking when he realized he held it. Livia ate something she had brought and offered Elias some.

“I'm not hungry.”

“You can keep it for when you notice.”

He accepted.

Vern stood near the window. There was not enough hatred in a tired man's posture to justify all the time Elias had spent imagining him otherwise. There had been a decision, and that was enough.

Livia put what she had offered into his hand without asking him to eat it at once. Elias held it as something to remember later. The corridor had high windows and light so uniform that looking at the floor could not reveal how much time had passed.

Etienne sat beside Edith. They were not discussing the miracle of recovery. The nurse merely asked whether he wanted her to stay within their agreed time. He nodded, then looked at the door without demanding a prediction.

Elias found it unfair that he could glean no hint from the way people emerged. He had finished answering, yet part of him kept believing silence was an additional examination on which he could lose marks.

Marcel rested the folder on his knees. Elias noticed his master's fingers were tired. He had seen the master work for him, speak on his behalf, and wait beside him on so many occasions that he had almost stopped considering the real cost of all that time and effort.

“Thank you,” he murmured.

“We haven't finished yet.”

“I know.”

The master looked at him and nodded, accepting that thanks need not await the outcome. Then he straightened the folder's edge again. He offered no promise about the judgment in return.

Elias stopped asking waiting for a recognizable shape. Keeping the place where they would find him was the only task until called. For several minutes he managed it without rising.

When they were called back, Elias forgot for an instant how the room was arranged.

The conclusion on the technical facts was read without emphasis. The preliminary measurements were not found erroneous for the reference in which they had been taken. The subsequent move and reuse of the certification had introduced a relationship not calibrated anew. Responsibility for that choice could not be assigned to Elias by pretending he had measured a different position.

He heard his name in the sentence and realized he was not breathing.

The provisional restriction confining him to workshop activities was to be removed. His qualification, rank, and the normal limits of the work he could undertake remained; no one handed him greater expertise as compensation.

They told him where to collect the notice needed to make the update usable. Marcel took note. Elias listened twice, not because he failed to understand, but because part of him still expected the sentence that would leave everything pending again.

It did not come.

For Etienne, recovered health did not erase lost work. The amended offer and the account of recognized days were handled separately from the reconstruction of the accident. The commission's office had arranged payment of that balance without requiring a false statement about the first measurement.

Other consequences for those responsible would follow the relevant proceedings. They were not pretended already completed to give the room a tidier ending.

The person reading the conclusion returned to the reference of the notice to collect. Elias followed with almost painful attention. The outcome would not remain in his hands merely because he had wanted it long enough: he must understand what update had been ordered and how to make it usable.

Marcel did not answer for him when asked whether he understood. Elias repeated the removal of the restriction and stopped before adding a qualification nobody had granted. The supernatural ability he possessed was the same one with which he had entered the room; the decision concerned his being able to resume work within the limits he already possessed.

Vern listened to instructions about parts requiring further acts. He sought no handshake to turn admission into total absolution. Etienne asked for the reference of the arranged payment and received an answer precise enough to follow outside the room.

Elias gathered his notebook. A page caught on the case's edge and needed two attempts to free. It seemed absurd that something so small could still be difficult when the greatest weight had shifted.

Livia made room. The room emptied without the solemnity he had imagined on previous nights. Another matter waited on the table and the listeners would continue their day. Their work did not diminish what had just been returned to him; it merely reminded him he need not possess the whole world to have a real conclusion.

Outside, Elias stopped before the wall.

Marcel waited.

“Is it over?”

“This part, yes.”

The sentence was enough. Elias bent slightly, hands on his knees, and laughed once with a sound he could not have repeated. Then tears came, and he no longer seemed to need to choose which was dignified.

Livia passed him a handkerchief without making it a moment to remind others of later.

He kept his palm against the wall until his body stopped seeking a grander gesture. The stone was cool in shadow. It neither forgave nor judged; it offered support stable enough to let him breathe.

“I don't want to cry here,” he said.

Livia left him the handkerchief without trying to hide it from others.

“Then we'll wait until you want to move.”

The answer removed his last self-imposed task: immediately living his release well. He need not offer brilliant gratitude to those who had worked with him. He could be tired, relieved, unable to laugh without hurting his throat.

Marcel asked whether he wanted more water. Elias shook his head, reconsidered, and accepted. The master did not point out the contradiction. He passed the water and waited until Elias could hold the glass steady.

Livia turned to let someone pass. She was not protecting the moment as though the entire building must remain motionless for him. Yet she stayed. Elias thought he would remember that presence more than the exact sound of the sentence removing his restriction.

He wiped his face and folded the handkerchief badly. He would return it clean, he said. The copyist laughed faintly without asking him to organize that debt immediately too. For now they could collect the necessary sheet.

They collected the notice before the office closed. The sheet had not become a spiritual protection; it restored something precise taken from him that an employer would have to recognize.

Etienne returned shortly afterward from the desk arranged by the commission. He had counted the money and received the receipt for the agreed days. Not a signature erasing the harm because he had been treated.

“It's come,” he said.

Elias did not ask how happy he was.

“Good.”

The worker looked toward the street.

“Now I'm going home.”

No one stopped him for a compulsory toast. Edith wished him a good evening and took her leave too, after agreeing the contact she wanted to maintain.

Vern emerged last. He passed Elias and slowed.

No handshake was necessary to make the conclusion valid. They exchanged a nod and went in different directions.

On the way back, Elias purchased only the time to pause before the instrument shop. He did not enter to turn relief into an unplanned purchase.

He let someone pass before the instrument shop's window and shifted enough to leave the entrance clear. For weeks he had imagined the conclusion would immediately return him a light body. He was still sleepy, hungry, with a lump returning to his throat whenever he thought of the sentence read aloud.

Marcel had told him when they would resume work. The next date existed without having to begin with defending his permit. Elias held to that detail as he continued along the street.

The sheet bore the necessary update. He did not need to hold it up and show it to every person passing in the street in order to make the decision valid. He kept it in its case, away from the edge rubbing his jacket. Caring for documents could become part of the day again instead of occupying every thought.

At the first crossroads he instinctively sought an opening sentence for home, before they asked the outcome. Long explanations came, qualifications about jurisdiction and everything the decision did not mean. Then he thought of Adrien widening his collar and Lise outside a door with her own fears.

They need not hear the whole record at once to understand something important had happened. He could set the notice on the table and begin with what changed tomorrow.

He walked more slowly. Time saved need not immediately prepare another defense. In the street were a wheel's sound, a bundle badly held beneath an arm, a conversation that did not end when he passed. He could see them without asking for a judgment on his history.

At home he realized he still gripped the case. He loosened his hold before knocking. He needed to enter with the sheet intact, not with the force he had thought necessary to keep its outcome.

The smell of a meal rose from the lower floor. Elias felt hunger return, and a tiredness he no longer wanted immediately to defeat. He raised his hand to the door, remembering he could be simply the son and brother coming home after a difficult day.

At home, Lise opened before he knocked a second time. Adrien rose from the table.

Elias placed the notice where it would not catch the lamp oil.

Lise looked at the sheet without touching it until Elias made room. Adrien removed his glasses, cleaned a lens, and resumed reading at the right place. Elias waited. He had imagined telling them a thousand times; now he could let the news arrive in their time too.

“Tomorrow I can go out to work again.”

Lise stayed beside the table with her hand on a chair's back. She did not ask whether everything was easy now. Elias saw she too was waiting to understand what he needed that evening and, for the first time, managed not to interpret the waiting as another question to answer well.

His father read the sheet, then looked at his son.

“Then tonight you can stop.”
