Helren returned on the twenty-fourth and began with the work Elias had promised.

Not with the threat concealed behind the sample, nor with Mael Vaur's name. With the scale drawing, the measurements, and the place where a line in the previous copy had turned a rough patch into a regular surface.

Elias had worked the agreed two hours; Marcel had checked the reproduction. The sheet carried no independent certification his permit did not allow him to sign.

Elias had cleared enough bench space to spread the plan without laying it on the sample. Before Helren's arrival, he had checked the margin so often that a small eraser stain had become intolerable.

Marcel had asked what made the sheet less readable.

“Nothing. But you can see it.”

“You can also see you erased a mistake.”

He had left the stain. Now he watched the inspector seek the point where the first copy had hidden the irregularity. He could no longer improve the plan by touching it; he must wait for the finished work to speak in its own form.

Ilian had arrived looking like someone who would rather be elsewhere but had decided to come anyway. He removed his coat only after seeing where to put it. Elias recognized the difficulty of entering a place where one's work risked being described as part of someone else's deception.

He offered him space beside the window. The draftsman did not thank him for being treated as innocent; he asked whether the light would interfere with the examination. Elias shifted the plan slightly. It was a concrete question, and therefore did him good.

Helren began at the reference indicated at the top. Following his gaze was impossible; Elias ended up rereading his own words, pretending not to seek the moment the inspector would find a flaw.

The inspector examined it in silence.

Ilian, beside the window, had brought the correspondence from his commission that he could show. He had not been summoned as an accused man. He had agreed to attend because his name risked remaining attached to a statement he had not produced.

“Here,” Helren said.

Elias moved closer.

“The original error is identified as such. Good. You haven't corrected it by making the version you started from disappear.”

The praise was so small Elias nearly failed to recognize it.

Helren accepted the work and placed the promised extract on the bench.

The authentic note, the one the inspector had written, requested identification of the sample before attesting a connection. In the subsequent version, that same request became a reference to a positive certification. It was not merely a wrong measurement.

Ilian read the passage twice.

“My drawing was going to be an attachment to this?”

“That's what the correspondence you've shown asks you to prepare,” Helren replied. “It doesn't mean you've already signed the statement.”

The draftsman leaned against the bench. His fingers closed over its edge without exercising Crucible.

Elias remembered the bill folded inside the notebook. Part of the payment was still on the other side of that request.

“If you refuse this form, you can still ask for the balance for the work you've done,” he said.

Ilian looked at him.

“Asking isn't receiving.”

Elias had no answer that bridged the distance.

Marcel separated the delivered drawing from the one Ilian had worked on for the client.

“I can attest the work you did here. Don't promise the rest to make something you couldn't verify look complete.”

Ilian was looking at his own plan without touching it. Elias understood this was not simply about claiming payment. The drawing was correct within the received assignment's limits, yet someone wanted it to represent a service the draftsman had never seen.

“Can you deliver this without the certification sentence?” he asked.

“I can propose it.”

The absence of greater reassurance prevented him from describing refusal as a choice the world had already rewarded. Ilian could lose the client and be left with an outstanding balance. He still needed to eat even if he was right not to sign.

Marcel said he would describe only the work done in his workshop. He neither offered to pay in the client's place nor urged a heroic gesture others could observe without bearing its cost.

The draftsman reopened his notebook. He scanned the request, seeking where he had accepted reproduction rather than a guarantee of operation. The page preserved what he had written at different times without making all those hours equivalent.

Elias thought of his restricted permit and how much he wanted someone to acknowledge the work he could still perform. Watching Ilian, he understood he must not turn that resemblance into forced intimacy. It was enough to indicate where Marcel's answer could make a distinction legible even to the client.

The draftsman followed the point with his nail. He asked Elias to hold the sheet while he rewrote a sentence. Elias did so without proposing another solution for a balance he did not possess.

“And if they go to someone else?”

“Perhaps they will. Your name doesn't have to follow them.”

The inspector showed the bond on the testimony and compared the extract with the document kept in custody. The relationship between them was not merely the resemblance of letters. An ordinary copy could reproduce the mark's design; that would not make it the same attestation.

Elias did not ask Helren to invest a greater certainty. He recorded what they could say: the text shown did not authorize the reference to certification. He did not record having seen Vaur falsify a sheet.

“What did they ask you to move?” Marcel asked.

Helren kept one hand on the folder.

“A group during a demonstration of the connection. I asked who they were, what consent they had given, and who was responsible for the operation. I received more descriptions of the apparatus.”

“Not names.”

“Not names usable for those questions.”

Ilian raised his head.

“I was supposed to draw a component.”

“And that's the work you've declared,” Helren said.

For a moment, the workshop's things lost their familiarity to Elias. A staircase, a bench, a door: ordinary elements could become part of an action no one showed whole to those hired to carry it out.

The Hinge did not leave its custody to help him understand. It was not even named before the inspector.

Marcel prepared the commercial reply: willingness to return the sample under the delivery agreement, examination limited to what had been observed, no offer of a working specimen, and no certification of the transit of people.

Ilian wanted to read the sentence concerning his work before it was copied.

He changed it.

Not a great alteration. He removed a word that might have made an approval requested by others seem his own. Elias let him finish without suggesting a more heroic formulation.

“I can carry the reply,” Ilian said. “Not the piece you haven't offered for sale.”

“The sample will be returned through the agreed delivery,” Marcel replied. “We won't use your willingness to skip that too.”

Ilian nodded. He folded the sheets with a care that did not make his face seem any less angry.

Ilian reread the altered sentence with the sheet nearly upright. The light clarified its margin; Elias could see where a letter had been rewritten not to resemble another.

“I hadn't understood the drawing would be presented like this,” the draftsman said.

Nobody answered that he should have understood. The distance between reproducing a component and attesting a connection was real even for someone who knew enough magic to understand that an object could lie through its form.

Marcel had him identify work already delivered and work the client had not yet received. Separating them did not pay the balance. It would let him request what was owed without retrospectively lending his signature to a service never performed.

Elias listened. He wanted to know the client would accept, that a correct answer would produce an orderly conclusion. Helren did not give him that prediction. He returned the comparison to the text he was authorized to provide and left the reader's reaction to the future.

Ilian put the sheet in its case, checking that no edge remained exposed. The same precaution as at their first meeting now seemed to Elias a gesture of obstinacy: preserving one's work well even when someone else had wanted to misuse it.

He moved to let him pass and did not detain him with a promise of indefinite help. Both had tasks to finish. The prospect of discussing it again would be more credible if neither had to pretend the meeting had solved everything.

Helren gathered the folder. The extract remained on the bench under the agreed limits of use, not the inspector's whole archive. No new name had joined the Exchange merely by entering the right room.

Once Helren had put the documents away, Ilian stayed beside the bench. The commercial reply was almost ready, and Marcel asked him to reread it without waiting for the page to be formally finished.

The draftsman shifted the sheet to avoid casting a shadow. The hands he had used to sense components now sought only a line. No ability could choose for him which sentence he would agree to carry to a client.

Elias observed his own name in the margin of the reproduction the master had checked. It was no new licence, no guarantee the world would stop misusing a plan. It acknowledged a specific job he had completed while the restriction remained in force.

Ilian closed his notebook and nodded to him. They had not promised friendship. They had spent a morning together and prevented a sentence growing larger than its support.

The master waited until the visitor left before returning the box to its place. The door stayed open onto the alley; ordinary work began asking for room again. Elias should have felt only relief, but instead felt a sharper unease too. Someone had tried to purchase a piece of certainty for a purpose they would not fully explain.

He gathered the reproduction sheet without opening the apparatus's case. Understanding the danger a little better did not oblige him to show it a shorter route.

When they were alone, Marcel asked Elias what they had obtained.

He looked at the reproduction, the reply, and the sample still enclosed.

“A reason not to hand ours over.”

“We already had that.”

Elias nodded. He took time to formulate the difference.

“Now we know someone tried to buy the missing part of a proof. And that the people caught in the middle don't have to keep going just because they've started.”

Marcel left the reply to dry.

Elias still did not know what the client would do when faced with refusal. For the first time, though, he did not confuse that ignorance with an obligation to open the door.
