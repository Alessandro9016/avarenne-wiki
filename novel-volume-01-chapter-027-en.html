Chapter 27 — The Papers Outside the House

The document did not smell of the baron's room.

The words were the baron's, copied by a hand that did not know their tone. Alexander sought Celine in the text and found her where a signature was meant to decide for her.

The court official put a finger on the reproduction's provenance. The originals had been acquired in Ravelle's study and remained in the office's custody. Edran followed the reference, then returned to the guardianship line.

He had not heard that sentence under Will. It had existed before Ravelle entered the room, when the baron still imagined a house without Alexander.

It was the twenty-fifth. In the intervening days the world had continued moving enough to produce a result without requiring him to enter every room.

The draft proposed a temporary administration. Not the transfer of the ducal title to the baron, nor a national vote obtained by proxy from a minor. It was subtler: make indispensable the hand that would sign beneath Celine's name.

Beside it were notes on appointments and contracts to review.

The official arranged the sheets so every reference could be found again. He had not brought the entire investigation as a gift for the duke. His work required distinguishing what he could show from what remained in custody, and Edran let him explain before reading.

The copy described a future Alexander would never have lived. A household after mourning, a minor surrounded by counsel, a man ready to make his own presence indispensable. The lettering was composed. Had the young man died, the paper could have appeared on a table with the same practical air with which Albin had presented the wages.

Edran stopped at a phrase. He had heard it spoken without malice so often: for the sake of continuity. He understood how little banning words from the house would accomplish. The problem was the place left to those who must live inside them.

“This line concerns his administration,” he told the official.

The man indicated where the plan proposed it as a solution, not a right already granted. The distinction increased his anger rather than reducing it. A young man had been killed for a project its author had not even made inevitable.

Alexander turned the sheet. The reverse offered no further signatures. He returned to the first page and asked which parts had been reproduced for comparison. The official showed him without guaranteeing what the delivered documents did not contain.

Edran took time to examine the margins too. Not to discover an occult sign: he wanted to distinguish what he read from what his imagination had already put in the baron's room.

Albin waited until the duke passed him the page. The administrator knew enough of the house to recognize where Ravelle's project began speaking of people as though they were merely available positions.

Albin read without quite sitting down. When he reached a reference to an estate matter, he placed his finger beside the line.

“This description is incomplete.”

“In what way?”

The administrator explained which decisions had already been delegated and which required separate consultations. The baron's project was not a mechanism that would work as soon as Alexander died. It contained errors, underestimated resistance, and people who had not promised to obey.

Edran felt a cold anger.

A man had been willing to kill even for a future he had failed to organize properly.

“The four counts haven't signed,” he said.

The representative confirmed only what he had delivered: the draft obtained bore no such signatures. He did not guarantee that he had collected every conversation held in the duchy.

The official let him examine the margin where endorsements should have appeared. He did not present it as proof nobody had held ambitions. It was merely empty in the document they could examine.

Edran thought of the cruel pleasure a complete list would have given him. A name could be sought, questioned, forced to answer. An absent signature instead asked him to tolerate unfinished work without deciding its limits insulted his authority.

“Is this the copy you are leaving me?” he asked.

The man checked the reference before answering. He showed him how to identify it without confusing it with the original. Edran listened. He did not want to ask Etienne to believe a page existed merely because he had seen it in his study.

Albin took a separate sheet for his corrections. He avoided marking the reproduction the court had brought. It was no solemn gesture: he wanted to preserve the difference between the baron's plan and his present explanation of the household's operation.

For a moment Edran could watch their two hands working without superimposing the murderer's. He realized anger was trying to take even his ability to recognize an ordinary contribution. The people before him had no duty to perform his indignation well enough to make him feel less alone.

He took back the copy when they finished. He set it in the clear space at the table's center and let the official finish explaining the next steps. His certainty did not need to interrupt to exist.

Alexander accepted the distinction. A single guilty group would have been convenient; convenience had not become more innocent because this time he would have enjoyed it.

Albin asked whether he could note on his own sheet the parts that did not match actual jurisdictions. The official distinguished explaining administrative operation from a statement about the baron's personal agreements.

“I am not telling you whom he spoke to,” the administrator specified. “This procedure would not have obeyed that signature merely because he had envisaged it.”

Edran watched the finger beside the line. A few days earlier, he could have made the same practical error in a benevolent decision, confusing personal wealth with a ducal function. The analogy did not equate intentions. It prevented him from treating imperfect procedural knowledge as sufficient reason to recognize a murderer.

“How many could have stopped him?” he asked.

Albin said he could not count objections that had never occurred. He could indicate where other decisions would have been necessary, and that none of those people appeared as signatories to the draft before them.

Alexander nodded. It was less comforting than imagining a household able to defend Celine like an organism without needing to know. People would have had to take responsibility for a conflict, and the world did not provide advance proof that they would.

He looked at the study door. A doctor, a provisions administrator, a friend had entered there. The baron had used the same normality. He did not want a future in which every visit began with suspicion, but he must learn to leave a door open without surrendering his judgment along with it.

Mirel arrived with the result of the examination of the residue. She had arranged for the portion intended for testing to be separated and the remainder preserved, documenting the steps. The report indicated toxic adulteration consistent with the observed harm. The shape of the glass could not tell her who had introduced it.

“This doesn't identify a hand,” she said before he asked.

“But we have more than my account now.”

“Yes.”

The official noted the report's reference. Mirel checked it before he copied it. She added no conclusion about motive. Edran let the doctor finish describing what the examination could support without granting it the ability to identify a murderer from glass.

The small vial remained a clinical object, distinct from the spent Quiet Glass fragments; nobody attributed a power to it to make the investigation more interesting.

Alexander looked at the papers together. The delivery. The contents. A statement obtained through imposed will. A draft that existed outside his own house and his own ability to create a copy from nothing.

It was not yet a judgment. It was enough to stop seeking a different murderer.

“Other people will need to be heard,” the representative said.

“Without treating kinship as a confession.”

The man looked up. He had received no invested command; Alexander also left him room not to be impressed by a duke's clarification.

“Each person's position will be considered separately.”

When the representative left, Albin remained with the sheet of his own corrections.

“Do you want me to review access for all the notables?”

“I want access to correspond to the work people need to do. Not an inherited entitlement to trust because a door was open to my father.”

“That will mean saying no to people who haven't done anything like this.”

“I know.”

Edran was not certain he yet knew how often. He would have to discover it by living in the house, not merely defending it.

Mirel gathered her notebook. Before leaving she asked whether he had rested.

“I haven't needed to.”

The doctor was not persuaded that the answer settled everything.

“That isn't the same question.”

Alone, Alexander went to the window. A gardener was adjusting the support for the branch that had tapped the glass. He had brought a second piece of wood because the first was insufficient.

Edran could have created a perfect support. He could have unmade the branch. There was no concealed moral significance in the wood requiring him to leave the work to the man.

That afternoon, however, he did not summon him to demonstrate anything.

The page intended for Etienne remained blank a few moments. Edran sought wording that would let the young man receive news without feeling called to choose between two people before opening the letter.

He began with the relevant findings. The draft had been found; he did not attribute a perfection to it that it lacked. The residue report had provided corroboration; he did not turn it into the memory of whoever had adulterated it. He wrote enough to make the update's purpose comprehensible and omitted the satisfaction part of him would feel at seeing his account confirmed.

He reread the recipient's name. In recent days Alexander's friend had become an accused man's son whenever someone spoke his surname. Edran did not want to help him disappear into that definition.

He stopped before adding a request to visit. Etienne had said he did not know whether he wanted to return. The letter could reach him without becoming a summons.

Outside, the gardener tested the new support and waited until the branch stopped swaying. He did not look toward the study to see whether the duke had watched. Edran took up the pen and finished his interrupted sentence. His attention too must learn to remain where needed without constantly asking the world to demonstrate that he had returned.

He finished the letter with the reference of the office that could confirm what had been acquired. He left the ink to dry and read the first line again: Etienne, not Ravelle. The son’s name had not changed, yet he already found it difficult to see it without the father beside it.

It was the same man. That was the part no evidence would make simple.
