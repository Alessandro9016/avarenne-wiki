Chapter 59 — The Full Tank

The bucket waited in the same place as the day before.

Elias saw it as he entered the Mutual's yard and had to walk around it, because someone had set it right where the passage between two crates ended. He could have moved it. He kept looking until Gaston asked whether he had found a new defect in the water.

“No. In the place.”

“You can fix that without the caliper.”

Elias picked up the bucket by its handle and carried it to the wall. He had slept enough not to feel tired and too little not to remember his empty hand after passing over the key. His jacket was dry; the shirt beneath it was a different one. Lise had not yet returned the one with the stained cuff.

Yselle came out of the pump room. She had already opened the folder on the high bench and had a pencil behind her ear. When she saw him, she did not ask whether he was afraid.

“Will you take the readings up top, or do you want Ruy to come?”

Elias looked at the service stairs. “I will.”

“Good. First come and look at the connection.”

The old joint remained on its cloth, moved to the side bench to keep it out of the way. The curved shield held its shape; it had not grown thin overnight, and the wall did not show through the metal. Pump number one was where they had stopped it. The replacement pipe section, held by its new supports, had the same color as the evening before.

Elias checked the points he had measured with Yselle. He touched the railing before climbing to the upper reference, without hiding the reason from himself. It was still there. The simple certainty of metal beneath his fingers let him look elsewhere.

Marcel arrived with Noemi and two operators who would need to run the machine. One had a broad hand with an old crushed nail; the other carried a short pencil in his breast pocket and kept looking for it even while it was already between his fingers. Elias knew the movement. It happened to him with the caliper.

“Today the floor doesn't drink before we do,” Noemi said.

“We haven't laid a place for it,” Yselle replied.

There were two chairs beside the dry wall. A young man arrived with a third and asked Noemi where it should go. She indicated the window, then sat in the one he seemed to have reserved for himself. He looked at her indignantly; she gestured toward the other, and he allowed himself to be persuaded.

Alexander entered with Teren once everyone was in position. He did not stand in front of the pump. He greeted them, asked Yselle whether anything was missing, and received directions to a free spot by the wall.

“You'll see better than we do from there.”

“That could become a problem,” the duke said.

Yselle handed him the reading sheet. “Then at least you have something to do.”

Elias climbed. From the walkway he could see the tank, the inlet, and part of the yard. He could not help locating everyone below as well. He found their positions, made a mental note, and returned to the water level. He did not have to hold everyone in his palm to read a height.

The first opening was small. A sound passed through the repaired pipe, lower than the one he remembered. Water took the circuit's route; Elias saw the surface in the tank tremble before he could distinguish the flow at the inlet.

“It's coming,” he said.

Gaston repeated the words toward the room. Not a shout. Information that could reach the person with a hand on the control without forcing him to look up.

Elias read the first reference. The pencil moved on the sheet below him. They waited, then Yselle called for the second opening. The sound changed, more continuous; no vein of water appeared along the pipe. Elias had imagined that, at the first noise, he would see only yesterday's failure. Instead he also saw what was different: the support's hold, the joint's seating, the pump body continuing its work.

There was nothing to hold up with power. The machine worked.

The tank filled fast enough to make him stop counting seconds in his head and use the agreed reference. He reported the new reading. Ruy asked him to repeat it because he had misheard the last part; Elias repeated it without climbing down or feeling contradicted.

At the next instruction they opened a service outlet. The surface dropped for a little while, then began rising at a different rate. A splash in the yard was followed by laughter. The woman who had carried the buckets the first day was holding her hands beneath the outlet.

“It isn't for washing your face yet,” Noemi told her.

“I'm not washing my face.”

“I know you.”

The woman filled a container and moved it. She had not needed to go down to the basin and climb back with the weight. The missing journey left her a moment without a task; she looked around as if someone might accuse her of standing idle.

Then she took a cloth and began another job.

Elias followed her with his eyes as far as the door. She had not stopped working. She had stopped lifting that water. The distinction entered his readings with a clarity he could not write beside the numbers.

The test continued through the conditions Yselle had specified. The operator with the pencil recorded each change on the sheet and asked for confirmation before writing down the reading. They stopped the pump when they needed to check the resting level, observed the return, and resumed with the circuit in the required arrangement. The new joint stayed dry. The wall soaked yesterday did not get wet again.

Alexander followed the reading sheet. At one point he showed Ruy a line he did not understand; the master came over and explained the difference between the two measurements. Elias could not hear the words. He saw the duke lower his head toward Marcel's finger, then look at the tank and nod.

When the main part was over, Yselle called Elias down from the walkway. He was in no hurry. He left the dry spot where he had kept his notebook, descended, and found himself beside the control where Noemi had needed the key the day before.

The operator with the broad hand moved the lever at Yselle's direction. The pump stopped. They waited for the return check, then the man himself prepared to reopen it. His movement was less fluid than the engineer's, but they did not need to take the control away from him. His partner wrote something on his sheet and asked him to do it again more slowly.

“If you do it on your own, I can watch your hands,” he said. “If you talk while you're doing it, I can't see anything.”

The man showed him the movement without comment. Then they changed places.

Alexander returned the sheet to Yselle. He told her he would be at the workshop and that, if she needed him again, Teren would know where to find him. Elias watched him leave the room, wait for a container to pass in the corridor, and depart on foot with his escort.

The water did not slow when the duke passed through the gate.

The young man with the chairs looked at the place he had left, then at the container filling. Noemi said something quietly to him. He smiled, but did not answer. He stayed to watch the flow as though he wanted to use all the time nobody was asking him to spend on the stairs anymore.

The shield could be removed. First they stopped and isolated the circuit in the way they had tested, leaving the room free of residual pressure. Two men from the Mutual brought ordinary equipment for moving the metal. Gaston cleared the passage and Marcel showed them how to distribute the weight.

Elias took hold at a dry point. The shield moved like a heavy object, not an apparition about to dissolve. They carried it onto supports prepared along the storeroom wall; the curved edge left a strip of floor that Noemi wanted kept clear. She would decide later with Yselle what use to make of it.

Without the shield the room seemed wider. For the first time Elias could see the whole repaired arrangement at a glance. He had associated that metal with protection, but its place would not always be between the people and the machine.

They restored the service. The woman returned with two containers, filled one, set it down, and moved to the second. This time she had no moment of confusion. She had already found a way to organize the rest.

Noemi went to check the upper floor with one operator. From the stairs she called Elias to join her: a door, opening, brushed the handle of the container left beneath the outlet. Together they moved the container and marked the more convenient position on the sheet. Nobody confused that obstruction with a defect in the pump.

“See?” Noemi said. “Now I have to teach them where to leave full buckets. Before, at least they took those away at once.”

Elias showed her the bucket he had moved on arrival. “I corrected one this morning.”

“Then you're ahead of the new work.”

Ruy was gathering the reading sheets when they returned. He compared them with Elias and left Yselle the copy for the file. The share collateral was not written on any of those lines, yet the fact that the tank was full changed what she could ask the next day. Elias knew that without claiming to understand the price of the change.

“Good,” the master said at last.

The master's word had not been followed by a correction. Elias waited another instant for one, then closed his notebook.

The operator with the short pencil came to find Elias before he left. He had copied the pump's number onto his sheet and wanted to know where to find the same reference on the drawing. Elias showed him the heading, then the detail distinguishing the machine from the Mutual's circuit.

“Can I keep this sheet here?”

“Yselle is leaving you the copy. The original goes back to the workshop.”

The man looked for a dry surface to put it on, found only the bench, and grimaced. There it would be under containers before noon. He called his partner, and together they chose a spot on the wall near the entrance, where it could be read without blocking the passage. They had an ordinary clip; Gaston found a board to fasten it to.

Elias waited while they decided how high to place it. The first position suited the tall man and was almost useless to his partner, who made him lower his arm without a trace of deference. They hung the board lower down. The paper stirred in the draft from the door, then stayed still beneath the clip.

“Tomorrow I'll remember I put it here,” the man with the pencil said.

“And the day after?” his partner asked.

“You'll be here to tell me.”

Elias added no advice. The sheet's place had become a matter for them.

A woman passed carrying a container in both hands. She came from the high outlet, without having climbed the stairs with the weight. She asked someone to open the door. Elias stood and held it until she had passed.

Behind the wall the pump kept sounding, steady and sufficiently uninteresting to leave room for voices.
