Chapter 64 — June Eighth

The man from the bank looked for the price on the machine before looking for it on paper.

Elias understood from the way he bent in front of pump number two. He was not checking its alignment; he was trying to find something in the metal resembling the work he had expected to finance. He had removed a glove to touch the edge of the base. His hand stayed clean.

“This one hasn't been put into service.”

“It was checked here,” Yselle said. “The one installed at the Mutual is number one.”

The man moved to the third. His folder already lay open on the dry bench they had left for him. Elias kept his own closed, waiting to know which sheets would be needed. Marcel, beside the window, watched the visitor with the patience he reserved for someone not yet finished asking the wrong question.

“And they belong to you?” the man finally asked.

Yselle took the donation signed the day before. “Here is their origin. Here the objects are identified. No stake in the project is assigned to the donor.”

The representative read. His sharpened pencil was much shorter than its holder suggested; he had to press twice to get it out. He marked a point in the margin of his own copy.

“I can't disburse funds for supplies you're no longer buying.”

“I'm not asking you to.”

Yselle showed him the new statement. Elias recognized the visible difference between the old total and the corrected one but did not try to read its meaning in the visitor's eyes. His own work was in another folder.

The man followed the column of canceled items. He passed over some quickly; at the prototype work he stopped and went back.

“These remain.”

“They've been completed. We keep them separate from those replaced by the machines already here.”

“The reduction doesn't settle previous commitments.”

“That's written in the proposal.”

Elias realized this was not an argument where the better-informed person could simply correct the other. The man from the bank knew how to read accounts. Yselle knew what she had ordered. Both wanted a document that would survive differing memories of the morning.

The representative turned the page. “An invoice is missing.”

Yselle searched the envelope of supporting papers, then called Martin, who was arranging copies in another part of the workshop. Her husband found the sheet between two drawings and passed it to her. A fold concealed its reference. The man opened it, compared the date, and put it with the expenses already committed.

“This work wasn't given by the duke,” he said.

“No,” Yselle replied. “And the people who did it shouldn't lose out.”

The representative nodded. Their first point of agreement concerned not the miracle but ordinary work that still deserved payment.

Then came the question for Elias.

“How closely do they match the drawings?”

He opened his folder. He had prepared the drawings for a technician; the visitor did not need every dimension named. Elias chose the point where the pump body joined the circuit and showed him the reference marked on number two.

“These are the dimensions checked. I compared the measurements with ordinary instruments. Here is the component that can be removed for maintenance.”

The representative moved closer but did not touch the catch. “Can you open it?”

Yselle looked at Elias. He put down the sheet and prepared the bench so nothing he removed would be lost. Machine two was disconnected; demonstrating access did not require stopping the Mutual's water. He removed enough to reveal the removable component, set it down, and let the visitor examine the seat. The existing seal stayed in place while he indicated the edge holding it.

“Can this be made locally?” the man asked.

“We can manufacture replacements from the known forms,” Yselle replied. “We already have some. We'll need to test their use with the operators.”

“Will you still depend on the donor?”

“For these objects, no. That isn't the service we're asking you to finance.”

Elias put the component back. The catch returned to its seat. His hand seemed slower than Martin's the day they had tested the instructions, but the operation succeeded without anyone needing to show him a hidden access point. The representative made a second note.

Marcel showed him his findings on the materials. He offered no universal guarantee for every substance the duke might create in the future. He spoke of the machines present and the checks completed, with the surfaces and instruments available. The man asked him to repeat a distinction between what he had observed and what would become apparent only in use; Ruy repeated it without irritation.

“The problem,” the representative said, “is that the previous financing covered a process. Now you're showing me existing assets and a different process.”

“That's why I wrote before the deadline,” Yselle replied.

The man looked at the reading sheet. “I want to see the service.”

The Mutual was close enough to walk. Elias gathered only the necessary sheets and went out with Yselle, Ruy, and the visitor. Martin stayed with the papers. The duke was not summoned to escort the bank: Elias had glimpsed him in the yard with Teren, discussing departure.

On the way the representative asked Yselle whether the other two pumps already had final destinations. She told him what was established and what was not. She did not turn the number three into three completed installations simply because he wanted to imagine them productive. The second and third machines were real assets available; their future installation work remained to be organized.

At the Mutual Noemi welcomed them with a bowl in her hand. She took it to the kitchen before approaching. Elias noticed she was no longer hiding her wrist, but the representative was looking at the corridor, not her skin.

“It's been working since yesterday?” he asked.

“The service began yesterday,” she replied. “Now you can see how we use it.”

The operator with the pencil took his sheet from the board on the wall. He did not need to ask Elias where it was. He brought the visitor to a spot where he could watch without obstructing the control and pointed out the tank above. Water was coming in; a woman filled a container and passed behind them.

“We used to carry it upstairs,” Noemi said.

The man from the bank looked at the stairs, then the container. He asked how often the outlet was used. Noemi showed him the morning's rhythm without turning it into a promise of income the Mutual had not made. What they saved was journeys, weight, time. They were not selling every bucket to a new customer.

The representative did not seem disappointed. He wanted to know who would handle interruptions and who would receive a request for a replacement. Yselle showed him the workshop's limited commitment and the instructions still to be completed with the operators. She did not pretend the successful test replaced everything left to learn.

Elias climbed to take the requested reference reading. When he came down, the man was examining the old fracture on the side bench.

“This is what broke.”

“The preexisting joint,” Yselle said.

“It's in your report.”

“Yes.”

The visitor turned the cloth to see the worn edge. He sought no saboteur in the metal; he asked who had made the repair and which section had been replaced. Elias pointed out the new section and supports. The distinction did not erase the failure but made it a fact they could discuss without blaming the wrong machine.

Before leaving, the representative returned beneath the tank. He let the woman pass and waited until her container was full. He made no notes until she had gone. Elias wanted to know whether that silence was a good sign; he decided not to derive a measurement from it.

They returned to the workshop shortly before noon. Martin had prepared a copy where previous commitments and new needs occupied two recognizable sections. The man read it and rested his pencil on the point Yselle had kept separate from the beginning: the share collateral.

“Your request is for their release.”

“Yes. The capital must be reduced to the actual need. I don't intend to leave the entire collateral from the previous plan behind different financing.”

The representative considered. He did not say the machines' arrival obliged the bank to do what Yselle wanted. He asked that the new plan leave no unidentified expenses open beneath the word support. She took the pen and defined the items without removing the work needed to make the service repairable.

Elias stopped following individual words when they reached conditions he could not assess. He could recognize an exact dimension and a clause referring to his report; he could not pretend to know what commercial risk was acceptable to the other two.

An argument drew him back to the papers. The representative had separated a receipt from the sheets of canceled expenses and held it beneath two fingers.

“This advance has already been used. It doesn't disappear with the order you won't place.”

Yselle looked for the reference on her statement. “It's in the section for commitments already made.”

“Here the cross-reference points to the supplies column instead.”

Martin bent over the sheet. The reference was wrong: while copying, he had followed the line above. He said so at once and moved the ruler so both could see the point. Yselle corrected the connection without reducing what remained to be settled.

The representative released the receipt. “This is what I need to take back. If the capital goes down, I must explain what remains in force and what will no longer be disbursed.”

“I'm not asking you to cancel money already used,” Yselle said. “I'm asking you not to finance what's already here now. And not to keep me bound for that need.”

Elias looked at the machine beside the table. The metal had not erased the prototype work, the amount advanced, or the earlier signature. It had removed the need to pay for a second construction. In the discussion the difference had finally found a place that did not depend on amazement.

The man from the bank asked him to move the test report closer because his own arm would not reach without knocking over the pencil holder. Elias did, and picked up the pencil that had meanwhile fallen. The man thanked him, then wanted to verify the attached document was the one shown at the Mutual.

Elias recognized a small water stain beside Ruy's signature. He indicated the date and the two requested readings. The representative put the sheets together in the new order, leaving the receipt in the section that still had to be honored. Yselle watched and finally released the edge of her statement.

At last the representative closed his folder only halfway. The sections to rewrite were marked, previous expenses acknowledged, new needs separated from those that had disappeared. They would prepare the final text for early afternoon, before the duke's departure. Yselle did not yet hold the release of her shares. Before her lay terms both parties were prepared to sign.

Ruy asked Elias to return the drawing beside pump two. He put it back and closed the caliper case. The machine looked identical to the one that morning. He knew that, among those sheets, its weight had just changed.
