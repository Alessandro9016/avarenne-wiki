Chapter 55 — The Reachable Section

The first sound Alexander heard after the valve closed was Noemi breathing.

She still had the valve key clenched in her hand. Water fell from the metal shield in an ever thinner sheet; behind it, the open pipe gurgled twice and went quiet. A washer spun across the floor, carried toward the drain. Alexander stopped it with his foot before it disappeared.

“Don't open it again,” Yselle said.

Nobody moved toward the valve. Teren stood at the door, letting the last man through into the corridor. Elias stayed beside Noemi until she had found a steady place to stand, then looked at his own hand as if he expected something to be in it. It was empty. The key had reached the place it needed to reach.

Noemi looked down at her arm.

Blood ran from her forearm to her wrist, mingling with the water. The broken edge of an old mounting bracket had sliced her skin when she turned toward the shutoff. The cut widened beneath the wet fabric as soon as she loosened her grip.

“Now I notice this,” she said, annoyed.

Alexander removed a glove.

“I can close it.”

Noemi looked at his hand, then his face. “The way you did with the metal?”

“A different way.”

She offered him her arm. “Then yes. Before someone scolds me about the floor.”

He supported her wrist. Contact gave him something precise: living tissue, small severed vessels, the recent break that the body was already trying to contain. Heart did not require pain to become solemn. Alexander invested restoration in the injury he had reached, without extending it to anything she had not asked him to touch.

The blood stopped welling up. The edges joined beneath his fingers; the skin regained its integrity so quickly that Noemi's next movement, bringing her sleeve up to dab at it, became unnecessary. She made half the motion and stopped. Water washed away a red streak. Beneath it there was no wound concealed or merely put to sleep. The cut was gone.

Noemi turned her wrist.

“Does it still sting?” Elias asked.

“No.” She pressed a finger where the flesh had been open a moment earlier. “Not anymore.”

Alexander released her arm. He had seen men wait months for relief he could have given in less time than it took to find a bandage. It was an old memory; the Mutual's corridor, smelling of soap and wet brick, kept it from becoming the whole of the present.

Yselle had watched without speaking. Now she offered Noemi a dry cloth someone had brought her.

“For the blood that's already come out,” she said.

Noemi gave a single short laugh and cleaned her wrist. “That doesn't go back in?”

“It doesn't need to,” Alexander said.

“Good. Because I wouldn't want it back.”

The laughter reached Elias too. His came out crooked and ended in a long breath. Only then did Alexander see his fingers shaking: the young man hid them by gripping the edge of his jacket.

“And you?” he asked.

Elias inspected himself from shoulders to feet with almost offended haste. “In one piece. Very wet.”

Teren came just far enough into the room to check it. “The corridor is clear. Nobody was injured.”

“Apart from this,” Noemi said, showing skin that could no longer prove it.

Alexander could have created a pipe to replace the broken one at once. He could also have created an entire line as far as the wall. He did not yet know what lay beyond, or how the section was secured in the masonry. The distinction lay before him, in the space between something within his reach and a city that continued beneath the stones.

“Show me the piece that needs replacing,” he told Yselle.

His aunt turned toward the joint. She did not look like someone returning to a failed endeavor; she had already begun to take the damage apart in her mind. She pushed back her sleeve and called Elias.

The new pump stood motionless on its base, intact. The failure was in the old service line, beyond the connection they had installed. The flange had lost its support when the joint gave way; a bracket, flaking and dark beneath its paint, now hung from a single anchor.

Yselle pointed without touching it. “First we take the weight off this. Then we open up enough to see both ends.”

“I can hold it.”

“Here.” She showed him the spot. “Not on the cracked part.”

Alexander reached the metal from the dry side of the shield. The weight offered him no difficulty. Elias brought a prop from the entrance, and Yselle placed it beneath the section that was still sound. They did not begin loosening bolts until the load rested on it.

The first bolt turned; the second resisted with a long, hoarse squeal. A man from the Mutual returned with two wrenches, asked whether they needed another pair of hands, and stayed to hold a container beneath the remaining water. It filled in silence. A flake of paint floated on the bottom.

Alexander let them work. The urgency was over, but his intervention still had consequences: the curved shield remained in place, resting with its outlet toward the drain, and obstructed direct access to the lower section. He moved to give Elias's shoulder room. The young man thanked him without looking up.

When the joint came free, Yselle laid it on a cloth instead of throwing it into the container.

“We keep this.”

The fracture ran around the thinned edge. Alexander saw a brighter surface where the metal had broken recently and an older band, slowly eaten away. The defect had a history from before their arrival. Now it had a name, a location, and witnesses too.

Elias measured the exposed ends, then passed the caliper to Yselle. She checked again. Not to challenge him: one measurement belonged on the drawing, the other to the shape Alexander would make into matter. They discussed access to the bolts and clearance for removing the replacement in the future. The pipe no longer had the right to be forgiven an unreachable bolt simply because it was old.

“We'll replace this support,” Yselle said. “And the one beside it. If the first has failed, I'm not asking the second to pretend nothing happened.”

Alexander crouched to look beneath the pipe. “The anchor in the wall?”

“We expose it and check it. We don't cover it up.”

The man from the Mutual put down the container. “Should I fetch the mason?”

“For that spot, yes.”

Noemi, now sitting in a chair brought from the corridor, told him where to find the man. Her voice had returned to that of someone with a day's work to put back together. Alexander was pleased by that before he knew why.

It did not take them long to make the local damage clear. Yselle drew the replacement on a dry board, marked its dimensions, and showed him the removed piece together with the sound surfaces. Alexander followed the configuration, the materials already agreed upon, the position of the seals and complete supports. He asked her to clarify a section the drawing left ambiguous. Then they moved the cloth holding the failed joint and cleared a rectangle of floor.

Cradle brought the shape into the world.

The new section appeared complete, its ends already defined, its metal continuous beneath the light from the doorway. Beside it the supports took substance with their respective fastening parts. A bolt rolled down the slope of the floor; Yselle stopped it with the toe of her shoe and picked it up. The known configuration became something they could lift, bring into position, install.

Elias stood with the caliper open in his hand.

“I prefer it when you at least announce you're about to do that,” he said.

“You're right.”

“Not because of the danger. I always feel as though I'm in the wrong moment.”

Yselle ran her fingers along the new fitting's edge. “I'm beginning to feel I'm in the right one.”

She did not thank him at once. Instead she asked them to turn the piece, checked the seat, and had them test access with the bulkiest wrench they had. The wrench fitted and could complete its arc. A smile changed her mouth before she straightened.

Installation took hands and space. The new metal did not bolt itself onto the pipe. Alexander held the replacement up while Elias guided one end and Yselle checked the other for alignment; the mason, who had arrived with dust still on his shoes, examined the now accessible anchorage. They replaced the damaged fastening. The supports were positioned and tightened, the seals checked in their respective seats. Elias gathered the last nuts from the cloth and passed them to Yselle. When she asked for a second brace of the same configuration, Alexander gave it to her.

The floor dried in patches around their feet. Teren sent one of his men to report that the duke would be staying at the Mutual a little longer, then took up a broom himself to clear the passage. Noemi showed him the better drain. Her authority over the room survived a duke's visit very well.

A boy looking in at the door asked whether he could have the bucket he had left inside. Noemi told him to wait a moment, then handed it to him herself, walking behind the new support. The boy stared at the bright pipe, but what he wanted to know was whether he would still have to carry water upstairs the next day. Yselle told him what time the test would begin. He stayed to count the trips that might disappear. Alexander saw the calculation pass across his face; it was worth as much as the newly born metal, and golden eyes were not needed to read it.

“The test tomorrow morning,” Yselle decided when the final tightening had been checked. “With the tank ready and everyone who will need to run it.”

Alexander looked at the closed line. The local damage had been repaired. The water could return; the room, meanwhile, still bore traces of what had happened: the cloth with the old joint, the soaked clothes, a dark stripe on the wall. They did not need to erase those things to make the result real.

“The shield can stay until then,” he said. “We'll remove it after the test.”

“And the metal?” the man from the Mutual asked.

Yselle looked at Alexander. He nodded.

“You keep it here. We'll find a use for it that doesn't involve saving our legs.”

Noemi stood. The cloth she had used was pink in one spot and gray in another. She carefully folded those parts inward. Alexander thought she meant to hand it back, but she kept it.

“What you did to my arm,” she said. “Can someone ask for that for something that didn't happen today?”

He did not look at her prosthesis before answering.

“Yes.”

The word came out simply. The possibilities it contained were not simple. Noemi looked down at the cloth, and for a moment she seemed more frightened than she had when the water struck the metal.

“There are people here who would ask for it,” she said. “And others who would like to think without someone waiting for them in the doorway.”

“We can find the time.”

“I'm not making appointments for everyone right now.”

“I understood.”

Noemi looked at him again. “I might write you some questions. I want to know what I can say without making promises on your behalf.”

Alexander told her where to send the sheet before he left. She asked him to repeat it, not because she had failed to hear, but to fix the address in the real day's business, among deliveries and food to be ordered. Then she called to someone in the corridor who had left a door propped open with a bucket.

Yselle wrapped the failed piece in its cloth and left the fracture exposed. Elias resumed taking measurements for the updated drawing. Alexander put his still damp glove back on.

When they left, Noemi's hand was already busy writing.
