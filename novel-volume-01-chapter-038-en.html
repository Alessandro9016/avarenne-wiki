Chapter 38 — An Open Door

On June first, Elias left with a folder rather than a defense.

The updated document was in its place. He did not check it at every crossing. At the workshop he found Marcel already busy and a job that had not waited for the hearing to become necessary.

Marcel indicated the prepared work and Elias began by asking which side had already been checked. It was the question he would have asked before the accident, but now he could speak without fearing that admitting ignorance would cost him the right to work.

The master passed him the instrument. He did not congratulate him again on the permit or prepare a task too easy to expose him to error. He left something within his competence, with the same possibility of requesting a check as yesterday.

Elias positioned the piece. Work had not changed during the hearing. It could still be done badly, and he did not want proof of innocence to become a reason to stop checking a measurement.

He looked at the wall behind the cabinet. They had replaced it after cleaning; the niche continued to exist unseen. He remembered the crossed-out nine left legible and how Marcel had allowed correction without turning the mistake into an identity.

A voice from the alley asked whether the workshop was open. The master answered and work continued. Elias did not wait for the new client to recognize his name or know the conclusion. The world could offer a morning in which he need not explain where he was returning from.

He realized he had reached the first useful measurement without opening the testing folder. He left it on the shelf and took up the instrument. For a few minutes he was merely a man trying to finish work well.

They worked for part of the morning without discussing the warehouse bench. Elias noticed only when Gaston entered and asked whether they could arrange a technical visit for the next day.

The request came from Yselle Valcienne's pump project in Lethorne. They needed a comparison of measurements and a usable drawing for part of the work. It promised no higher source, requested no secret object be taken from the workshop, and did not replace discussion of time and payment.

Elias read the name. He knew enough to recognize an important family, not enough to know the person or her private interests.

“June second,” Gaston said. “First we look. Then we decide the work.”

Marcel asked whether he wanted to participate within his own competence. It was not an invitation to Astrolabe II, nor an order to be grateful.

Elias accepted.

The visit was arranged. It had not already happened. He liked discovering he could want something that did not serve to prove he was right about the past.

That afternoon Livia arrived with a small parcel. The bread was less fresh than she wanted, the marmalade bitter enough to be hers without a label.

“To celebrate?” Elias asked.

“To eat. You can do the other thing too.”

They had agreed on a short meeting with Edith. Not everyone present would stay, and no one would have to recount their day to justify the right to leave.

They reached Room Six by the ordinary door with Marcel's consent. They did not consume the remaining imbued opening. The room was still too large for four people and comfortable enough to let them avoid filling it merely to feel important.

Elias placed a copy of his professional update on the table. Livia did not make it a shared trophy. She retained it only for the comparison and storage he authorized, while the original remained at his disposal.

Marcel discussed the backlog of work. Edith asked how Etienne was and did not seem surprised he had gone home instead of following his own recovery from one meeting to another.

Edith sat near the side she had chosen at the first meeting. She made no exclusive claim to it; she placed her bag so she could retrieve it without asking someone else to rise.

Elias looked at the table. The first time he had imagined more voices, pseudonyms spoken naturally, requests immediately finding the right person. Now there were bread, a useful sheet, and a woman who had spent part of her day off returning.

“Do we have to discuss something important?” she asked.

“Not necessarily,” Livia answered.

Marcel smiled. He had a workshop to reopen and enough experience to know that a meeting ending at the agreed time left more possibilities for the next.

Edith asked whether the box had been returned to its place. The master nodded. The test had not consumed the hall's availability or turned the room into a machine activated at every entry. They could keep coming through the door while seeking someone qualified to replenish it.

Elias indicated the professional update and briefly explained the change. He did not present the result as the circle's victory over an enemy faction. They had collaborated on a concrete problem; others had done their own work outside this table.

Livia sought the updated notice among the papers. Elias passed it over without taking out the whole case. “This says where I can work.” Edith checked the date and put it back on the table. The clinical extract remained with the hearing documents, outside the folder he would leave with the group.

When the folder closed again, he felt how much room remained in the meeting. For the first time in weeks they could use that time without discussing the measurement that had brought him here.

“Have we obtained what we began for?” Elias asked.

Livia looked at the sheet, then at him.

“Part of it. Your permit wasn't the only reason to seek people who could speak without belonging to someone.”

Elias nodded. The sentence did not take away his victory. It left it a place small enough to contain the others' lives too.

Edith cleaned the bread from her fingers and put down the napkin.

“I'd like to participate regularly.”

For once, surprise stopped him answering before she finished.

“Not at every meeting. And not as someone who must be available if anyone gets hurt. I want to be able to seek help too, and decide which services I accept.”

Livia asked what she wanted to keep outside their shared agreements. They discussed shifts, patient information, and the fact that the clinic would not follow her person like a bottomless bag.

No one asked her to choose Heart to deserve a place.

Elias gave his assent. Livia did the same after they had read together the conditions Edith corrected. Marcel remained the external technical guarantor: they did not need to make him a founder to thank him for letting them work.

Edith reread the availability sentence. She had removed a term that could make her intervention seem automatic whenever a member requested assistance. Livia asked whether the new phrasing matched what she wanted to accept.

“I want to be here even when I'm no use for treating someone,” the nurse said.

Elias nodded. He could have answered that they already considered her a whole person, but that would ask her to take comfort from their intentions. He preferred to let her alter the agreement until it could be read without guessing their affection.

“And you can ask for something too,” he said.

Edith leaned back. She had no immediate request to prove joining had been rational. Perhaps she would want information, a service, or someone with whom to examine a problem. The right to return did not depend on knowing already.

Marcel asked which contact to maintain for tasks concerning the place. He remained outside the group as naturally as he had helped it. Elias thanked him without adding a title that would force the master to become responsible for all their future decisions.

Edith chose a place from which she could reach her bag without making Livia rise. Elias began moving another chair to give her room; she stopped him with her foot.

“This one's fine.”

He left it. On the table, between bread and folder, the request sheet remained. None concerned his hearing.

“Next time I'll bring something I need,” Edith said.

Livia turned the sheet toward her. “About time.”

They were three members now. Four people at the table.

Edith did not immediately choose a pseudonym. She could do so when she had reason. Her own voice sufficed to say yes.

“I want to learn to open it myself,” Elias said before gathering the papers.

Marcel looked toward the antechamber. “That isn't the next exercise.”

“I know.”

“And it isn't something you try because you have an evening free.”

“I know that too.”

Livia turned to him. “Are you angry with the people who answered us, or do you truly want to learn?”

Elias had both answers. He had seen the passage's two sides, felt the distance he could not place between his hands. He wanted to return without counting openings left by someone else, understand enough not to have to choose between keeping it closed and entrusting it to a stranger.

“I want to learn,” he said.

Marcel stayed silent a moment. Then he returned his notebook. “Then come tomorrow and see how Yselle works. And finish your part properly.”

Elias put the visit sheet above the other pages. Edith asked the time of their next contact, and Livia recorded it before discussion could return to the hinge.

When they left, Elias waited until everyone had collected what belonged to them. Marcel locked the door with the key already in his custody.

Livia took a different street from Edith. His mentor returned to the workshop. The network did not need everyone to finish the day in the same place to continue existing.

Elias opened his notebook in the street. The sheet for tomorrow's visit bore Yselle Valcienne's name and the work on the pumps. Until a few hours earlier he had chiefly counted the days until his fear might end. Now he had to prepare to enter an unfamiliar workshop.

He turned the page. There was the incompatible dimension of the returned sample. The question about the client had stayed after the sample left.

On the blank sheet he wrote two things to ask Marcel: who could replenish the Hinge, and how to meet them without handing it over. Then he closed the notebook. He had no answer yet, but this time it was a question he wanted to take beyond the workshop.

Across the street a cart passed, hiding the door they had left for an instant. When it reappeared it was closed, ordinary, among the others.

Elias headed home. On June second he had work; afterwards, a room to return to. Before reaching the corner he looked back once more. He hoped that door would keep looking like the others.
